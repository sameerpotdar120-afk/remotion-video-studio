# Horsefly short: our own script (v1)

Our own words and our own angle. The reference ("How Horseflies Bite You", 75 words, 27.6 s) covers the mouthpart
mechanics. This version adds verified facts the reference does not use: only females bite, the eye-gap tell, the
blood pool, meal size, and zebra stripes. None of the reference's phrasing is reused.

## Fact sources
- Only females bite (protein for eggs); males feed on nectar; males' eyes touch, females' are separated:
  Wikipedia "Horse-fly"; Tabanidae encyclopedia entry; backyardnature.net (Jim Conrad).
- Knife-like mouthparts slice the skin and the fly feeds on the blood pool that forms: University of Kentucky
  Entomology, "Horse Flies and Deer Flies" (EF511). Two pairs of cutting blades plus a sponge-like part that laps
  blood: Tabanidae encyclopedia entry.
- Anticoagulants in horsefly saliva: Kazimirova et al. 2001 (four species) and 2002 (19 species), Pathophysiology of
  Haemostasis and Thrombosis; Xu et al. 2009, *Tabanus yao* salivary proteome (PMC2742439).
- Meal of about own body weight: *Tabanus fuscicostatus* blood meal averaged ~110 % of unfed weight (LSU entomology).
  Hence "some horseflies".
- Zebras: Caro et al. 2019, PLoS ONE 14(2): e0210831. Flies landed on zebras less than a quarter as often as on
  horses, and failed to slow down. Hence "might be why".
