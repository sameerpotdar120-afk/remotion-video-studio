## Why it looks better than ours

1. **Every transition is motivated by an object.** The magnifying glass slides in, finds the fence, then *grows until
   its lens is the screen* (zoom through the lens). The dingo silhouette on the map *becomes* the full-colour dingo as a
   blue wash spreads out from it. The dingo *jumps off the treadmill* towards the kangaroo. No plain dissolves.
2. **Three looks, each with a reason:** the satellite map (place and scale), a blue blueprint board (the animal and its
   numbers), a sepia parchment map (history). The look changes when the story changes time.
3. **Real-looking cut-outs with cartoon accents:** a photo-real hand and magnifier, a realistic dingo, small and big dog
   for scale, a treadmill; then a cartoon kangaroo with googly eyes, sheep, settlers, sailing ships. Everything has a
   soft drop shadow and pops with overshoot.
4. **Numbers are always visual:** a km counter riding the fence line, a weight badge, a speed counter that climbs as the
   dingo runs faster, a comparison panel (London–New York) slid in beside the map.
5. **Continuous camera on the map** with a strong grade: saturated teal sea, warm desert, crisp coastlines, a fence line
   that glows (blue for the fence, red for the old fences).

## What we can't take from it

Its satellite basemap and all its cut-outs (hand and magnifier, dingo, dogs, treadmill, kangaroo, ships, settlers) belong
to someone else. We rebuild: EOX Sentinel-2 plates with our grade, the fence line from open data, the parchment map from
Natural Earth drawn in a sepia style in code, and every character either drawn in code or as the creator's own images.

## Our adaptation (proposal, for the creator to decide)

- **Keep the reference's structure and its object-driven transitions:** south-up flip, the magnifier that becomes the
  screen, the fence drawing with a km counter, the states lifting, the London–New York comparison panel, the silhouette
  that becomes the dingo in a wash, the treadmill and the leap, the whip into the parchment history map, the red fences
  joining and turning blue, the two sides filling.
- **Map work (our core):** EOX plates for Australia and the North Atlantic, a closer plate on the fence, the fence route
  drawn from open data (OpenStreetMap's barrier=fence way, ODbL, or traced from the official route map), states from
  Natural Earth; the parchment look built from the same Natural Earth coastlines.
- **Assets:** the realistic dingo (and the two dogs for scale), the hand with the magnifier and the treadmill need real
  images (the creator's own); the sheep, settlers, ships, traps, fence posts and kangaroo can be drawn in code in the
  cartoon parchment style.
