You've never heard of the longest fence on Earth, but 5,614 kilometers of it cut Australia in two.

Look closer. No, not there. There it is.

It runs through three states: South Australia, New South Wales and Queensland. That's longer than London to New York.

And it was built to keep out just one animal. This one: the dingo.

It's a wild dog, about the size of a medium dog you'd have at home.

It can sprint at almost 50 kilometers an hour, and for thousands of years it hunted kangaroos and other small animals.

Then, in 1788, Europeans arrived. And they brought something new: sheep.

For a dingo, that was the easiest meal in Australia.

Farmers fought back with traps and guns, but they kept losing sheep. So they started building fences.

By the 1940s, those fences were joined into one giant barrier.

Sheep country on one side. Most of Australia's dingoes on the other.

So who's really fenced in here: the dingoes, or the sheep?
