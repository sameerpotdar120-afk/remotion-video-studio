# Reference: "Why was Thailand never colonized?"

1080×1920, 30 fps, 113 s. English male VO, about 290 words (about 2.6 words a second). The video ends on the treaties line, with no CTA.

Analysed at 6 fps (678 frames) for the beats, plus 30 fps strips (every 1–3 frames) through each moving element for the timing and easing. Word timings come from faster-whisper medium.

  - White bold names with a thin dark stroke, rotated along the land ("French Indochina").
  - Small bitmap-style sticker labels ("Siam", "Burma").
- **Captions:** one word at a time in a yellow comic font (Bangers-like) with a black stroke, at y ≈ 78%. The stressed words are bigger.

- **Country characters:** country silhouettes with googly eyes, a small mouth, stick legs and stick or cartoon arms (the countryball idea, but using real outlines).
- **Pacing:** a cut or new element every 1–2 s. The camera holds still on the map most of the time, with quick zooms between regions.

## Fact flags (to check before our script)

- **"Never colonised":** Siam was never formally colonised, but it lost about half its claimed territory and signed unequal treaties. Keep that nuance.
- **Burma:** three Anglo-Burmese wars, 1824–26, 1852 and 1885. Burma was run as a province of British India until 1937, which is a real India link.
- **The 1896 Anglo-French declaration:** it guaranteed the Chao Phraya basin. The 1904 Entente Cordiale split the spheres (west British, east French).
- **"The King just waved his hand and gave it all away":** this is oversimplified. Siam ceded the Laos lands east of the Mekong after the 1893 Paknam gunboat crisis, further areas in 1904, and western Cambodia (Battambang, Siem Reap) in 1907. In 1909 it ceded Kedah, Kelantan, Terengganu and Perlis to Britain. Kings Mongkut (Rama IV) and Chulalongkorn (Rama V) modernised the state and played the powers off each other; the reference gives them no credit.
- **"East of the Salween":** Siam gave up claims to the trans-Salween Shan areas (about 1892). This needs wording care.
- **Treaties:** Burney 1826, Bowring 1855, Franco-Siamese 1856 and 1867 all check out.
- **Siam → Thailand:** the name changed in 1939.
- **The fighter jets:** an obvious anachronism, played as a joke. Ours can be gunboats (the real 1893 crisis).
