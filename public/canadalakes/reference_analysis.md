## Facts checked (Nov 1)
- Lake count depends on the size cut-off: about 880,000 lakes over 10 ha (ECCC estimate), "over two million" if very
  small water bodies are counted. Say "करीब 20 लाख, छोटी-बड़ी मिलाकर".
- Geographic Board of Canada created by order in council, 18 December 1897 (today's Geographical Names Board of Canada).
- Athabasca: Cree, commonly "where there are reeds". Mistassini: Cree, "large rock". Nipigon: Ojibwe (Animbiigoong,
  "at continuous water"); meaning less certain.
- Lake Louise: named for Princess Louise (Queen Victoria's daughter).
- Another Lake and And Another Lake: both official, British Columbia (BC Geographical Names). "This Lake": no official
  record found, so it is left out.
- Stupid Lake: official, Manitoba (near Shamattawa). Crotch Lake: Ontario (North Frontenac); named for its shape.
- Big Ass Lake: Halifax County, Nova Scotia, named 5 March 1953 (per Wikipedia citing NRCan).
- Pekwachnamaykoskwaskwaypinwanik Lake: northeast Manitoba, Cree, "where the wild trout are caught by fishing with
  hooks", 31 letters, Canada's longest one-word official place name.
- About 90,000 of Manitoba's lakes have no official name (Atlas Obscura; the date of that count is not confirmed).
