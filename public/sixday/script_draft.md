# Six-Day War (1967): our first long video, with 6 Shorts built inside (/yt-script)

## The reference (analysed at 30 fps)

Kings and Generals, "Six-Day War: 1967 Arab-Israeli War": 1920×1080, 29.97 fps, 15:58 (28,722 frames). About 14:10 of
it is story; the rest is the channel ident, the sponsor (Skillshare, which we drop) and the credits. I read it from
timestamped contact sheets every 2 s, full-size frames of every distinct look, and a scene-cut scan of every frame at
the native 30 fps.

**How it's edited**
- **One continuous 3D map.** There are only 26 hard cuts in 16 minutes, almost all in the intro, the sponsor segment
  and the archival inserts. The whole war is told by a camera gliding, tilting and pushing over one tilted 3D map:
  - Egypt green, Israel blue-teal, Jordan orange, Syria pink, Lebanon yellow, Saudi Arabia brown.
  - Painted terrain, rivers, gold serif place names.
- **Units as 3D blocks.** Dark tiles with flag-coloured icons for infantry, armour and air. They slide along the
  fronts, collide, and leave small explosion puffs.
- **Overlays:**
  - Portrait cards on stands (a framed photo with a name plate) standing upright on the map.
  - Radio-wave arcs between capitals for diplomacy.
  - Yellow attack arrows, and green target brackets on airfields.
- **Numbers:** stat cards with icons (soldiers, tanks, jets, side by side), and a losses scoreboard with flags at the end.
- **Breathers:** an opening "desk diorama" (old maps, a rifle, shell casings and small 3D tiles of jets and tanks),
  B&W photos on paper, and short archival film inserts.
- **3D flourishes:** jets swooping low over the map, explosions, and a final wide shot with the captured land hatched.
- **Grade:** muted, painterly colours, a strong vignette, film-grain texture.

**What we keep, and what we change**
- **We keep** the one-map-world storytelling, the unit blocks, portrait cards, stat cards, arrows and the calm, clear
  pace.
- **The voice is ours.** We tell it in Hindi, with our own structure.
- **India angle:** the 14 Indian UNEF peacekeepers killed in Gaza.
- **Hooks:** a hook in the first 15 seconds (the reference spends 56 s on ident, sponsor and preamble).
- **Shorts:** six self-contained Shorts are written into the script.
- **Grain:** no film grain (house rule) unless you want it.
- **Archival images:** none of the reference's footage or photos. Breathers will be our own illustrations (labelled
  AI) or verified public-domain photos with credits.
- **Tone:** neutral, military-and-map history. No religious framing, no blame. The topic is an interstate war, not a
  Hindu-Muslim subject.

## Fact notes (checked 21 November 2026)

**May 1967**
- The Soviet warning of Israeli troops on the Syrian border was later shown to be false.
- Egyptian troops moved into Sinai from 14 May, parading through Cairo.
- U Thant agreed to Nasser's demand to withdraw UNEF on 18 May; Egypt took the UNEF post at Sharm el-Sheikh.
- The Straits of Tiran were closed to Israeli shipping on 22–23 May.
- Jordan and Egypt signed a defence pact on 30 May.
- Israel formed a unity government on 1 June (Dayan became defence minister), decided on war on 4 June, and struck
  on 5 June.
- Israel's stated 1957 position: closing the straits would be a cause for war.

**The fighting**
- **Operation Focus, 5 June from 07:45:** about 190–200 Egyptian aircraft destroyed in the first wave (sources give
  189 or 197), so the VO says "kareeb 200". Jordanian and Syrian air forces were hit the same day.
- **Indian peacekeepers:** 14 Indian UNEF personnel were killed in Gaza (with one Brazilian), still awaiting
  repatriation when fighting began; Captain Vijay Sachar was among them (Indian parliamentary record, Wikipedia's UNEF
  list).
- **Sinai:** the Abu Ageila attack under Sharon; the Egyptian retreat order on 6 June; the canal reached by 8 June.
  Sinai is about 60,000 km², roughly 3× pre-1967 Israel (about 20,700 km²).
- **Jerusalem:** the Old City was entered on 7 June, and Jordan withdrew across the river.
- **Golan:** assault on 9 June, taken by 10 June, ceasefire that evening.

**Results**
- **Losses** vary by source: Israel about 776–983; Arab states about 15,000–20,000. The VO uses ranges.
- **Territory:** Israel took Sinai, Gaza, the West Bank, East Jerusalem and the Golan, more than tripling the land
  under its control (pre-war about 20,700 km², plus about 67,000 km²). Sources say "doubled" or "tripled"; the
  arithmetic supports "more than 3×".
- **The Yellow Fleet:** the canal was closed from June 1967 to June 1975. 15 ships were trapped (14 in the Great
  Bitter Lake, 1 in Lake Timsah). The crews formed the Great Bitter Lake Association and held the 1968 "Olympics".
  Only the Nordwind and the Münsterland sailed home under their own power.
- **Afterwards:** UNSC Resolution 242 (22 November 1967); the Khartoum summit's "three no's" (August–September 1967);
  the 1973 war; the 1979 Egypt–Israel treaty; Sinai returned by 1982; Israel withdrew from Gaza in 2005; Israel
  still holds the West Bank and the Golan.

**Left out on purpose**
- Contested claims and casualty incidents with disputed accounts.
- Any religious framing.

**To confirm before the edit**
- Israel's "~15 km" narrowest width is the commonly cited figure (about 9 miles, near Netanya–Tulkarm).
- The UNEF Indian contingent's size: the VO says only "ek bada dal".
