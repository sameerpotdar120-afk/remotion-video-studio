# Ram Setu reference: look, fact check, our angle

Reference: "Ram Setu YA Adam Bridge", an IQ-branded Hindi Short, 56.4 s. Transcript: `reference_transcript.txt`.

## The look

- Opens on a dark grey satellite map with **India filled bright red** and a big white "INDIA" label, then a fast zoom
  south to Rameswaram. The rest of the world stays desaturated, so red India is the only colour on screen.
- **Pamban island in yellow**, a "2.3 km" measuring line from the mainland, then a small photo card of Pamban bridge.
- Dhanushkodi gets a red pin and a photo card. "Ram Setu ✓" / "Adam's Bridge ✕" as big tilted labels.
- The map turns into a bright blue/green "Bay of Bengal / Indian Ocean" view; Ram Setu is a dotted white line with
  "48 km" on it; "It is not deeper than 1 meter" as plain text.
- Then a dark studio card: a rock photo with "7000 Year", a sand photo with "3000 Year", arrows.
- Ends with a cartoon of the vanar sena building the bridge, a Ramcharitmanas book cover, a 3D bridge render, and Shri Ram
  over a red India map.
- Clear colour code (red India, yellow island, blue water), fast zooms, small photo cards. No captions, no loop, no
  host. Images and the book cover are third-party; we can't use any of them.

## Fact check

| Claim | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| Pamban island is 2.3 km from the mainland, joined by a bridge | **About right.** The road bridge is about 2.3 km; the new rail bridge (India's first vertical-lift sea bridge, opened 6 April 2025) is about 2.08 km. |
| Its last point is Dhanushkodi; the chain runs to Sri Lanka's Mannar Island | **True.** Dhanushkodi to Talaimannar (Mannar). |
| One side is the Bay of Bengal, the other the Indian Ocean | **Loose.** North is Palk Bay / Palk Strait; south is the Gulf of Mannar. |
| The sea is no deeper than 1 m | **Wrong as stated.** Most of it is 1-10 m deep; some sandbanks dry out. ISRO's 2024 map found narrow channels up to about 3 m along the crest. |
| 48 km long | **Wrong / outdated.** ISRO's 2024 ICESat-2 study (Scientific Reports, Dandabathula et al.) measures about 29 km from Dhanushkodi to Talaimannar; Britannica says about 30 km. |
| The stones are 7,000 years old, the sand under them 3,000, so the stones were placed: man-made | **Not supported.** No peer-reviewed study shows this. It traces to a 2017 TV promo. Published dates vary by method and sample (radiocarbon on corals and shells, OSL on sand: from about 3,500 to 18,400 years). ISRO's 2024 study says its findings corroborate a natural, geological origin. |
| The scriptures mention the setu 7,000 years ago | **Not established.** The Ramayana's date is not fixed by any agreed method. |
| It is a man-made bridge | **Not supported by science.** It is a matter of faith for many. GSI and ISRO describe a natural chain of limestone shoals. In 2022 the government told Parliament that satellite images can't say directly how it formed or how old it is. A CSIR-NIO dating study approved by the ASI in 2021 is ongoing. |

Extra facts for our version (checked):
- **ISRO 2024:** 10 m resolution map from NASA's ICESat-2 laser data (Oct 2018-Oct 2023); about 29 km; 99.98% under water;
  ridge about 8 m above the surrounding seabed; 11 narrow channels.
- **1480:** Rameswaram temple records say it was walkable until a storm in 1480 (a record, not proof; Britannica gives
  the same "records suggest").
- **Dhanushkodi, 22-23 December 1964:** a cyclone hit; the Pamban-Dhanushkodi passenger train was washed away with
  about 115 people on board; about 1,800 died on Pamban island; the town was declared unfit to live in. Today a few
  fishing families live among the ruined church and station.
- **Names:** in the Valmiki Ramayana the setu is built by Nala; British maps called it Adam's Bridge.

## Our angle (the Mumbai formula)

An Indian place everyone knows (Ram Setu) + "you didn't know" (it was walkable until 1480; it is 29 km, not 48; ISRO
just mapped it) + people and emotion (Dhanushkodi's 1964 night, the train) + respect for faith and science both + a loop.
We never call it man-made or "just sand": science says natural, faith says Shri Ram, and the research is still going.
