## Accuracy notes (so our version is better)
- Iceland's name: the saga sources (Landnámabók) credit Flóki Vilgerðarson (Hrafna-Flóki), a Norwegian Viking, who
  climbed a mountain after a hard winter and saw a fjord full of drift ice. Earlier visitors called it Snæland (Snow Land) and
  Garðarshólmi. The reference's "they arrived in winter and saw only ice" is a simplification.
- Greenland: Erik the Red was exiled from Iceland for killings (c. 982), spent his exile exploring Greenland, and named it
  so that people would want to go there; Íslendingabók (c. 1120s) says exactly that. He then led settlers there (c. 985).
- Greenland's ice sheet covers about 80% of the island; Iceland's glaciers cover about 11% of it.
