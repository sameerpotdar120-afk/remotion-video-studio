# Reference analysis: "The Difference Between the House and Senate (U.S. Congressmen vs Senators)" (114.3 s)

Measured from the file: 720×1280, 30 fps, 3,428 frames, 114.3 s, English VO (transcript in `reference_transcript.txt`).
Scene detection finds only two hard cuts (2.4 s, 82.4 s); everything else is one continuous camera over a satellite
map, plus a blue blueprint-grid "org chart" board that the map slides into and out of. Captions: a small white line at
y≈70 % all the way through. It is not a loop: it ends on a House and Senate hemicycle filling with seat counts.

## Two worlds, and how the video moves between them

1. **The satellite map** (North America → Washington DC → New York / Wyoming): slow push, zooms, labels in a serif
   font ("NOVEMBER 3", "Washington, D.C."), handwritten yellow words and circles, white people icons, flags.
2. **The blueprint board**: a blue grid background with a cut-out photo White House and Capitol, orange boxed labels
   ("HOUSE OF REPRESENTATIVES", "SENATE", "GOVERNORS"), rows of white person icons, big yellow numbers (435, 100, 26, 1).
   The chart grows down the page as the story goes on; the camera tilts down and pans across it.

Transitions between them: an ink/paint wash (21.6–22.6 s, the map dissolves into blue in a blotchy mask), a side
wipe where the board slides in from the right like a second panel (61.8–64.8 s and 87.6–89 s, the map and the board
side by side for about 1 s), and a vertical slide.

## What we can't take from it

- The film and TV stills at 6–10 s, the president photos on the White House card, and the satellite imagery are
  someone else's. We rebuild: EOX Sentinel-2 for the map (CC BY 4.0), the US Capitol and White House as the creator's
  own images, people icons, flags (donkey and elephant drawn in code or as the creator's images), hemicycles in code.
- No real politicians' photos.
- The end hemicycle counts look like live results. If we show the 2026 results, they must be the real final numbers
  on the day we render; otherwise show the seat totals only (435, 100) with no party split.

## Facts to check before the script

- 2026 midterm election day: 3 November 2026 (first Tuesday after the first Monday). ✓
- House 435 seats, 2-year terms; Senate 100 seats (2 per state), 6-year terms, about a third up every 2 years. ✓
- New York has 26 House seats (since the 2020 census, from 2023); Wyoming has 1. ✓
- The reference's "100/3 = 35" is shaky arithmetic: 100/3 is about 33. In 2026, 35 Senate seats were up (33 regular
  plus 2 special elections, Ohio and Florida). Say "about a third" or "35 seats this time", not "100/3 = 35". To check.
- New York population ≈ 20 million (2020 census 20.2 M) ✓; Wyoming 588,753 in the reference: the 2020 census says
  576,851, later estimates are higher. To check which figure to use.
- A law needs both chambers and the President's signature (or a two-thirds override). ✓
- Governors head states, aren't part of Congress and don't report to the President. ✓

## A note for our version

There is a real India link, with a twist: Lok Sabha seats go by population like the House, but Rajya Sabha is not
like the Senate. Its seats are also split roughly by population (Uttar Pradesh has 31, small states have 1), while
every US state gets exactly 2 senators. It's the creator's call whether to use it.
