Here's how Congress actually works.

This is the White House, where the President works. And just down the road is the Capitol, where Congress meets.

Congress has two chambers. The House of Representatives, with 435 members. And the Senate, with 100.

In the House, seats go by population. New York has 26 districts. Wyoming, with its small population, has just one.

Each district elects one representative, and every seat is up every two years.

So the big states dominate the House. And that's exactly why the Senate exists.

In the Senate, every state gets two senators, no matter how many people live there. 50 states, 100 senators.

Senators serve six years, and about a third of the seats come up every two years. This November, it was 35.

To pass a federal law, both chambers have to agree, and then the President signs it.

That's why this election mattered. Whoever holds both chambers decides which laws ever reach the President.

So should half a million people in Wyoming have the same Senate vote as 20 million New Yorkers?
