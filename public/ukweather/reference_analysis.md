## The shape and the rhythm

A question hook, a weather-montage gag with a chibi Brit, the "rainiest place?" false lead, a myth bust (Colombia), then
the real answer as a map battle of five air masses that builds to a busy end card that loops. 15 sentences at ~165 wpm.
Every caption phrase has a visual change on its first syllable; cuts land on stressed words ("Look", "Actually", "But",
"From the west"). Nothing is ever still: the camera always drifts or pushes 2–4% per second.

One map look for the whole video (no style changes): a **satellite basemap** with a warm/teal grade (land olive-green,
deserts sand, sea deep teal with visible sea-floor relief), slightly desaturated, soft vignette. Countries are painted
with **flat fills that keep the terrain texture** (multiply-like): UK brick red-brown (#8B3A22-ish), Ireland grass green
(#5E9E2E), Colombia orange (#E8901E). Overlays are bright and saturated on top: arrows, labels, glows.

## Style rules to keep

- One satellite look, grade warm-teal, terrain always visible through the fills.
- Country fills flash cream first, then settle into their colour (a "photo develop" look) with a small scale pop.
- Every title types in letter by letter, sometimes with a neon glow; the big ones fly in huge and shrink, or fly out at
  the camera.
- Arrows are thick, dashed, semi-transparent, with big triangle heads; colour = air mass temperature (blue cold, red warm,
  yellow dry continental, purple returning polar).
- Weather is shown as full-screen layers: rain streaks, fog, sun bloom, hail, wind curls.
- A recurring chibi character reacts to each beat.

## Facts the reference states (checked; to fix in our script)

- **"up to 250 rainy days a year":** parts of western Scotland and western Ireland see 250+ "rain days" (days with
  ≥ 0.2 mm or ≥ 1 mm depending on the definition). The UK average is about 150–160 days with ≥ 1 mm.
- **"more than 70 countries receive more annual precipitation than the UK":** the UK's average is about 1,150–1,200 mm
  a year (World Bank / Met Office ~1,160 mm). This is plausible, but the exact rank depends on the dataset; use "दर्जनों
  देश" or "70 से ज़्यादा" only if checked.
- **"first place usually goes to Colombia (3,240 mm/year)":** in the World Bank data Colombia is at or near the top
  (about 3,240 mm), with São Tomé and Príncipe and Papua New Guinea close. OK as "अक्सर सबसे ऊपर".
- **The air masses:** the UK Met Office describes six: Arctic maritime, polar maritime, returning polar maritime,
  tropical maritime, tropical continental, polar continental. Correct.
- **The Gulf Stream / North Atlantic Drift** warms the waters off the British Isles; prevailing westerlies bring moist
  Atlantic air. Correct.
