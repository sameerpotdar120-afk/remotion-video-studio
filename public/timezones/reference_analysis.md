## Look

- **The map:** one satellite world map, flat Mercator, treated as a sheet of paper in 3D. It tilts and rolls on a textured royal-blue backdrop (about #3A5FE0) and dives in and out of close-ups. The imagery is a commercial satellite basemap, which we don't copy; ours is EOX Sentinel-2.
- **The zones:** translucent coloured vertical bands with thin white edges. They show either as straight meridian bands or as the real, wiggly zone boundaries.
- **Labels:**
  - Offsets sit in a yellow row at the top edge (+1 … +12, −1 … −11).
  - The 0 zone gets a blue badge.
  - Glowing labels mark places: red for Greenwich, purple for "(Coordinated Universal Time)", and blue text on a yellow fill for France.
- **Transitions:**
  - Fast zoom dives.
  - Two black-vignette dips (3.4 s and 5.8 s).
  - Pins that pulse in red rings.
  - A dark night fade at the end.

## Fact flags (to check before our script)

- **"24 time zones":** that's the textbook figure. Real zones are about 38 because of the :30 and :45 offsets.
- **France, "12 time zones":** this is the usual figure, and 13 counting the Antarctic claim. France does have the most.
- **China, "5 time zones":** China used five zones from 1912 to 1949 and has used one (UTC+8) since 1949. Xinjiang also unofficially uses UTC+6.
- **Kashgar "still dark at 10 AM":** this holds only in winter. Kashgar's sunrise in Beijing time is around 9:30–10:00 in December, so we'd say "सर्दियों में".
- **Phoenix:** the straight −7/−8 line would sit at 112.5°W, which cuts through Phoenix's far-west suburbs. Arizona also skips daylight saving, which the reference doesn't mention.
- **LA, "UTC−8":** this is true in winter; it's UTC−7 in summer under daylight saving.
