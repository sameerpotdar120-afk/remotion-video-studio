## The shape

A question hook, a funny-name reveal, the "why", the setup (1953 border), the gag escalating (flag + bottle, flag + bottle),
a fake-out ("declared war?"), and the peaceful payoff (2022 split). 10 sentences, ~150 wpm, no intro, no CTA; it ends on the
new border so it loops back to the hook's map.

Five looks, each swapped with a flash/colour-wash transition, never a hard cut:
1. bright satellite world map (hook, the island close-up, the 1984 Greenland map);
2. a newspaper prop (the nickname);
3. a light grey "atlas" map with orange/yellow labels (the distance, the island pinned);
4. an old parchment map (1953);
5. a photo-cutout "sticker" diorama on a real Arctic beach photo (the flag-and-bottle gag), then old photo prints, then a 3D
   model of the island.

## Style rules to keep

- One look per act, swapped with a colour-flash or burn, never a hard cut.
- Typed labels (letters appear one by one) in yellow boxes, numbers that tick up on every distance and percentage.
- Sticker-collage gags: white-outlined cutouts thrown in with spin and a landing wobble.
- Restless camera: dives with radial blur onto the island at least three times.

## Facts the reference states (checked; to fix in our script)

- **The order is reversed.** In 1984 Canadian soldiers went first (planting a flag and leaving Canadian whisky); Høyem's
  Danish visit, flag, schnapps and "Welcome to the Danish Island" note came later the same year (28 July 1984 is given for the
  flag). The reference tells it Denmark-first. Our script should say Canada first, or not claim who started.
- **"about 4,000 km apart":** Ottawa–Copenhagen is about 5,600 km; Canada's east coast to Denmark is closer. Better: drop it or
  say "हज़ारों किलोमीटर दूर".
- **1953:** Denmark's 1953 constitution made Greenland an integral part of the Kingdom (no longer a colony).
- **The border:** the 1973 continental-shelf treaty drew the Canada–Greenland line but left a gap at Hans Island.
- **18 km:** the Kennedy Channel there is about 35 km wide, and the island sits about halfway, so "about 18 km from each side"
  holds.
- **2022:** signed 14 June 2022 in Ottawa; split along a natural ridge, about 60% to Greenland/Denmark, 40% to Canada; it gave
  Canada and Denmark a land border (their first). Denmark's parliament ratified it in December 2023. The island is about
  1.2–1.3 km².
