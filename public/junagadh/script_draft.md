## Fact notes (checked 15 November 2026)

- Junagadh was a princely state in Saurashtra with a Hindu majority (about 80%) and a Muslim ruler, Nawab Mahabat Khan III.
- The Nawab signed the Instrument of Accession to Pakistan on 15 September 1947 (Pakistan accepted it in mid-September).
- Junagadh had no land border with Pakistan; it was surrounded by Indian territory, with the Arabian Sea on its coast.
- India imposed a blockade; the Arzi Hukumat (provisional government) was formed in Bombay on 25 September 1947 and took
  over parts of the state.
- The Nawab left for Karachi in late October 1947.
- On 9 November 1947, at the request of Dewan Shah Nawaz Bhutto (father of Zulfikar Ali Bhutto), India took over the
  administration.
- Patel visited Junagadh and Somnath on 13 November 1947; the decision to rebuild Somnath followed; the rebuilt temple was
  inaugurated in May 1951.
- Referendum, 20 February 1948: in Junagadh's main territory, 1,90,870 votes cast, 91 for Pakistan (about 2,22,000 votes in
  all, including the vassal states; 130 for Pakistan in total).
- On 4 August 2020 Pakistan released a new political map showing Junagadh and Manavadar as Pakistani; India rejected it.
