## Style notes

- One continuous camera over a flat satellite world map (Blue Marble look) with slow drifts between beats, with two exits: the space/emoji insert and the 1870s film insert.
- A new visual every 1–2 s. Graphic language: soft glowing colour blobs for water masses (warm pink-orange, cool cyan), glossy 3D icons (fan, cloud, sun, cracked tile, thermometer), thin white wind streaks, white lasso ellipse, curved text on paths.
- Text: big bold sans titles with coloured glow for key terms ("El Niño", "Super", "2027"). Small white 2–3-word subtitles at about 71% of the height.
- Real data on screen twice (sea-temperature anomaly), which gives it authority.

## Audio (measured)

- Voice −15.1 LUFS, music+SFX bed −30.7 LUFS: the bed sits about **15.6 LU under the voice**. That's louder than the Diomede/Sentinel references (19–21.5 LU).
- Big hits (50 ms windows, relative to average speech level): El Niño title slam −5 dB (9.2 s), trade-winds whoosh −4 dB (16.2 s), ships −5 dB (21.8 s), thunder/rain −4 dB (28.8 s), winds-weaken whoosh −6 dB (35 s), **mind-blown explosion +0.3 dB** (43.6 s, the loudest moment), crowd/ambience −7 dB (44.5–45.3 s).
- Music: steady, low, slightly tense pulse under the whole video.

## Our version

- Same beat order and the same visual language, our own Hindi script (`script_hindi.txt`, 11 lines, about 78 s).
- **Our extra element: India.** After the world effects: "और भारत?" The camera flies to India, monsoon clouds over the country empty out, and a counter shows the 2026 monsoon at 13% below normal, the weakest since 2015. The famine beat is retold for India (1876, more than 50 lakh deaths in India alone).
- Real NOAA data instead of a screen recording: our heat maps come from NOAA OISST v2.1 daily anomalies (public domain), including a June → October 2026 time-lapse of the Pacific warming up.
- Effects montage trimmed to Peru, the southern US and Australia, to make room for India.

## Fact check (sources)

| Claim in our script | Check |
|---|---|
| Trade winds blow east → west across the tropical Pacific; push warm water west; cold upwelling off Peru | NOAA ENSO basics. Correct. |
| Sailing ships used them for centuries | Manila galleons, Acapulco → Manila, 1565–1815. Correct. |
| Every 2–7 years | NOAA. Correct. |
| Typical El Niño: +1–2 °C | NOAA (threshold +0.5 °C; moderate to strong +1 to +2 °C). Correct. |
| Peru floods, southern US heavy rain, Australia drought | NOAA ENSO impacts maps. Correct (southern US = winter). |
| India's monsoon often weakens in El Niño years | IMD; most Indian droughts since 1871 happened in El Niño years, but not every El Niño year is a drought. Hence "अक्सर". |
| 2026 monsoon 13% below normal, weakest since 2015 | IMD release of 1 Oct 2026: 87% of LPA (759.4 mm vs 868.6 mm); Business Standard, ETV Bharat, The Wire. 2015 was also an El Niño year (86% of LPA). |
| 1876: El Niño and monsoon failure, famine, more than 50 lakh deaths in India | Great Famine of 1876–78: India excess deaths estimated at 5.6–9.6 million (8.2 million in a recent demographic estimate). The drought is linked to the 1876–78 El Niño. |
| Could become the strongest El Niño on record | NOAA CPC, 8 Oct 2026: 83% chance for Oct–Dec of exceeding every event since 1950. Said as "बन सकता है". |
| Eastern Pacific more than 3 °C above normal | NOAA CPC 8 Oct 2026: Niño-1+2 already +3.9 °C, Niño-3 +3.0 °C; WMO projects Niño-3.4 around +3.7 °C for Oct–Dec. |
| 2027 could be the hottest year on record | WMO annual-to-decadal update (Hermanson): the late-2026 El Niño raises the chance that 2027 sets a new record; Hausfather/Dessler estimate about 1.76 °C for 2027. Said as "बन सकता है". |
