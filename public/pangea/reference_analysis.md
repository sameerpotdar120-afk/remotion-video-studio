# Reference analysis: "What If Pangea Came Back?" (62.6 s, GEOallday)

720×1280, 60 fps, English VO (`reference_transcript.txt`). Two hard cuts (22.0 s into the Zealandia close-up, 35.4 s to
the ocean map); the rest is one flat satellite world map with camera moves and fills.

## The look

- Flat Sentinel-style world map; a 3D globe on a dark-blue starfield for the title ("What if Supercontinent came back?",
  a fire-text "PANGAEA" with flexing arms).
- Pangea as an orange silhouette with "250 MILLION YEARS AGO" / "120 MILLION YEARS AGO" ticking.
- Continents in colour fills (Asia orange, Africa green, Europe yellow, Americas blue, Antarctica lilac), labels in
  bold white type, then the continents **slide together** into one landmass with a glowing outline.
- Zealandia close-up with a blue New Zealand fill and a glowing title; Australia red with an arrow.
- An ocean map in bright blue with Arctic/Atlantic/Indian/Southern Ocean labels that tilts in 3D.
- Country fills and pins for new neighbours (United States–Morocco with red pulse rings, Australia–Southeast Asia,
  Canada–Portugal–Spain), round icon badges (Land, Resources, Borders), crossed-out plane and ship icons.
- Ends on a FOLLOW ring around the globe (no loop).

## Fact check

| Claim | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| Pangea, a single landmass "hundreds of millions of years ago" | **True.** Assembled about 335 million years ago, broke up from about 200 million years ago. The on-screen "120 million years ago" is wrong for Pangea. |
| "Pangea coming back" | **Loose.** Pangea won't return; a *new* supercontinent is expected in about 200-250 million years (models: Pangaea Proxima/Ultima, Amasia, Novopangaea, Aurica). |
| Sea levels would plummet | **Speculative/oversimplified;** dropped. |
| Zealandia "could rise back above water", "almost the size of Australia" | **Wrong.** Zealandia is about 4.9 million km² (Australia about 7.7 million) and 94% is under water, mostly deep; a sea-level drop wouldn't raise it. Dropped. |
| US–Morocco, Canada–Portugal/Spain borders | **Fits one model** (Pangaea Proxima/Ultima, where the Atlantic closes). Used, hedged as "एक model के हिसाब से". |
| Australia merging with Southeast Asia | **True as a projection** (Australia moves north about 7 cm a year). |
| Disputes, trade routes collapse | **Humans almost certainly won't be around in 250 million years;** dropped for the real science below. |

## What we use instead (sources checked 12 Nov 2026)

- Farnsworth et al., *Nature Geoscience* (Sept 2023): in about 250 million years the next supercontinent (Pangaea
  Ultima) would see temperatures of roughly 40-70 °C over much of it, leaving only about 8-16% of its land habitable for
  mammals (one summary gives 8-25%).
- India: in Pangea it sat against Africa (via Madagascar), Antarctica and Australia; it broke away, raced north and began
  colliding with Asia about 50-55 million years ago, raising the Himalaya. It still moves about 5 cm a year to the
  north-east, and the Himalaya rises by more than 1 cm a year (Geological Society).
- Zealandia: about 4.9 million km², 94% submerged (GNS Science).

## How we match it

House satellite map (EOX Sentinel-2 + relief), Natural Earth continents (India's official boundary), the plates moving
backwards to Pangea and forwards to the future supercontinent drawn in code (continent outlines rotated on the sphere), a
heat map over the future supercontinent, India's trip north with the Himalaya rising, Null in the middle, loop ending.
