# What made the Mumbai Short go viral, and how Pangea uses it

Mumbai seven islands: nearly 3M views on YouTube, 1.5M on Instagram (creator, 12 Nov 2026). 66 s, 28 short lines.
Read from the video frame by frame (1 fps, plus the first 6 s at 4 fps), its transcript, and `exports/viral_analysis_mumbai.md`.
No retention or share data was available, so this is a reasoned read, not proof.

## What the video does

1. **The opening is an Indian place everyone knows, turned upside down.** "जिस मुंबई में आज दो करोड़ लोग रहते हैं,
   दो सौ साल पहले वहाँ समंदर था।" A familiar thing, a big number, and a contradiction, all in 6 s. Its hook only scores
   46 on hookscore; the topic did the work, not the wording.
2. **The first frame is India, then a fast push-in.** Frame 0 is the whole of India on satellite. By 1.5 s the camera is
   on Mumbai. Every Indian viewer recognises frame 0.
3. **Numbers move on screen.** A population counter ticks up to 2,00,00,000, then a "−200 साल" counter rolls back while
   the city turns into islands. The number moves while the voice says it.
4. **Satellite means today, old parchment means the past.** At "दो सौ साल पहले" the satellite dissolves into a parchment
   map. At "आज भी" it goes back to satellite. Viewers always know when they are without being told.
5. **Every name the VO says lights up.** कोलाबा, बॉम्बे, मज़गाँव, परेल, वर्ली, माहिम: one gold fill, label and arrow per
   word, about one a second. That rhythm holds attention, and people from those areas comment.
6. **Cut-out props pop onto the map.** Koli fishermen, a temple, a Portuguese ship, a dowry chest, a crown, a £10 tag, a
   storm cloud, the governor, a sealed letter that turns into "सस्पेंड!", a crane. A new object every 2–3 s.
7. **One continuous camera.** Only three hard cuts in 66 s (38.1, 38.8 and 52 s). The map never leaves the screen, so
   there's no moment that feels like a new video starting.
8. **Big years act as chapter cards.** 1534, 1661, 1782, 1784, 2024 in a large serif face, each on its own beat.
9. **A story with a turn and people in it.** Seven islands → given away → a governor defies the company → 7 becomes 1
   (the "7" counter flips to "1") → "पर सबसे ज़्यादा किसने खोया?" → the Kolis. It ends on pride and a sense of injustice.
10. **Short lines, steady speed.** About 2.5 words a second, one phrase per caption, so it works with sound off.
11. **The ending lands on the title.** "…जिनकी देवी के नाम पर शहर बना।" and the word "मुंबई" appears over the city.
12. **No host on screen.** Null never appears. The map is the whole video.

## The formula in one line

An Indian place everyone knows + "you didn't know this about it" + numbers that move + one name lit per word + a new
prop every 2–3 s + one continuous camera + people and emotion at the end.

## What Pangea takes from it

- **India is the hero, not the planet.** The story is "where was India, how did it get here, what happens to it next".
- **Familiar Indian things:** the Himalaya, Everest, and the shaligram many Indian homes worship (an ammonite fossil from
  the Tethys sea). That's the Kolis/Mumba Devi beat: something people hold dear, inside the geology.
- **Moving numbers:** "+5 cm/साल" ticking, a "−30 करोड़ साल" counter rolling back, then "+25 करोड़ साल" rolling forward,
  "15 cm/साल" speedometer, "40°→70°" thermometer, "8–16%" land meter.
- **Time code:** satellite = today, parchment = the past, parchment with a red heat grade = the future.
- **One gold fill per name:** Africa, Antarctica, Australia, Madagascar, Tethys, Himalaya, Atlantic, America, Europe.
- **Props:** Everest peak with a fossil, a shaligram, an ammonite, a crash flash, a thermometer, mammal silhouettes that
  fade out.
- **Shock line in the Mumbai shape:** "भारत कभी Asia में था ही नहीं।" like "मुंबई कभी एक शहर था ही नहीं।"
- **One camera:** continents slide under a single moving camera; no hard cuts except the loop seam.
- **Null:** Mumbai had no host. Recommendation: skip Null this time, or keep one clip of 4–5 s at most at the midpoint.
