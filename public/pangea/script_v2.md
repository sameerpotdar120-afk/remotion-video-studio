## How the edit copies Mumbai

- One continuous camera; no hard cuts. Continents move under it.
- Satellite = today, parchment = past, parchment with a red heat grade = future.
- Every number counts on screen while it's said.
- A gold fill for every place named, on the word, about one a second.
- A new prop every 2–3 s (counters, speedometer, fossil, shaligram, ammonite, thermometer, land meter, mammals).
- Big serif face for "−30 करोड़", "−5 करोड़", "+25 करोड़", "PANGEA", "PANGAEA ULTIMA".
- Phrase captions, white, low-centre, like Mumbai.
- Null: skip this time (Mumbai had no host), or one clip of 4–5 s at 0:48 at most.

## Fact notes

- India moves about 5 cm a year to the NE; the Himalaya rises about 1 cm a year (Geological Society).
- Pangea formed about 335 million years ago and broke up about 200 million years ago; "kareeb 30 crore saal pehle" is fine.
- In Gondwana, India sat next to Madagascar/Africa, Antarctica and Australia.
- After leaving Madagascar (about 90 million years ago) India moved at roughly 15–20 cm a year, the fastest known plate
  motion of its time.
- India met Asia about 50–55 million years ago, closing the Tethys sea.
- Everest's summit rock (Qomolangma Formation) is marine limestone with fossils of sea creatures (trilobites, crinoids).
- Shaligrams are ammonite fossils from the Kali Gandaki river in Nepal, from the Jurassic Tethys sea.
- Pangaea Ultima is one model of the next supercontinent (Atlantic closes). Others (Amasia, Novopangaea) exist, hence
  "ek model kehta hai".
- Farnsworth et al., Nature Geoscience 2023: 40–70°C over much of the land; 8–16% habitable for mammals; about 250
  million years from now.
