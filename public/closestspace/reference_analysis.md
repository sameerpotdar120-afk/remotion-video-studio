# Reference analysis: "Which Country Is Closest to Space? Can Everest Answer"

84.3 s, 720×1280, 30 fps, English VO with one-line lower captions. Read at 4 fps across the whole video and at 15 fps
(every second frame) on every transition: 0–1.3, 3.7–5.0, 10.4–11.7, 15.5–16.8, 21.6–22.9, 24.6–25.9, 28.9–30.2,
32.2–33.5, 36.6–37.9, 46.3–47.6, 59.0–60.3, 64.3–65.6, 72.5–73.8, 81.4–84.3. Transcript: `reference_transcript.txt`.

## The shape

One question, a wrong answer, a twist, the right answer. 18 sentences, about 150 words a minute, no channel intro, no
CTA at the end (it stops on the answer card, so it loops).

| Act | Time | What the viewer learns | Where we are |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–6.4 | which country is closest to space? "seems simple, or maybe not" | flat world map, tilted into 3D |
| Wrong answer | 6.4–28.4 | highest point = Everest, 8,848 m; split between Nepal and China | magnifier → 3D Himalaya flyover → border map |
| Twist | 28.4–59.3 | "but that's wrong": Earth isn't round, it's a geoid (spin bulge, lumpy mass) | dark space globe, flat-earth gag, geoid model |
| Right answer | 59.3–84.3 | equator radius +21 km; Chimborazo, 6,263 m, Ecuador; 2 km farther from the centre than Everest | radius diagram → equator dive → Andes → globe answer card |

Three scene families, each with its own look: (1) a bright, saturated satellite basemap (flat map) for "where on Earth",
(2) a 3D photoreal terrain flyover for the two peaks, (3) a 3D globe floating in a teal-green-to-navy nebula with faint
stars for the physics. Cuts between families are always a fast radial zoom blur (4–6 frames), never a hard cut.

## Beat by beat

**0.00–1.30 Hook map.** Flat equirectangular satellite map (Americas, then sliding right to Africa/Europe). Within the
first 0.3 s small white pins pop on peaks in every continent (Rockies, Andes, Alps, Kilimanjaro, Himalaya, Australia).
From 0.6 s each pin rises on a dashed white stem, the stems growing upward like balloons let go, while the camera pans
right (0.0→1.3 s, ease-in-out) and the map starts to tilt back.

**1.30–3.10 Tilt + title.** The map plane tilts to about 55° (a sheet in space with a thin white edge line and a faint
blueprint grid behind/under it); the dashed stems now read as vertical lines going "up to space". "closest to SPACE"
(white, bold rounded sans, "SPACE" bigger, on a soft blue glow band) is laid in the map's own perspective plane,
slides in from the right (1.5→1.9 s) and settles. Antarctica's white ice fills the bottom third.

**3.10–4.00 "it seems".** Hold, slow drift.

**4.00–5.40 "SIMPLE".** "SIMPLE" in green with a big green bloom slams in from top-left (0.33→0.67 s of the beat,
overshoot then settle), the title fades behind it and the stems fade.

**5.40–6.80 "or maybe not".** The same word flips to red with a red bloom and gains a "?" (SIMPLE?), wobbles, and at
6.75 punches up in scale (1.0→1.25) as the scene cuts.

**6.80–10.70 Magnifier.** Camera untilts back to a flat Old World map, zooming in. A photoreal magnifying glass (chrome
rim, black handle, lens magnifies ~1.6× with a refraction edge) enters from bottom-right and sweeps Africa → Arabia →
India, hunting. At "that's Everest" (9.75) it lands on the Himalaya; a dark label "Mount Everest" with a red triangle
pin pops inside the lens.

**10.70–11.20 Dive through the lens.** The lens scales up to fill the screen (0.3 s), the label grows with it, then a
radial zoom blur (4 frames) into 3D terrain.

**11.20–15.50 Everest flyover.** Photoreal 3D Himalaya (Google Earth in the reference: not usable for us), camera
gliding forward and tilting up from top-down to a near-horizon view. "9,000 meters" as a caption, then a floating label
"8,848 meters" (white bold on a soft cyan glow) with a thin leader to a red triangle on the summit, at 12.25–15.5.
Sky and haze at the top. "roof of the world".

**15.50–17.00 Summit to top-down.** Radial zoom blur (0.33–0.6 s) straight down onto the summit, landing on a top-down
satellite view with a small "Mt Everest" label and red triangle.

**17.00–21.60 The border.** A yellow line draws itself along the border through the summit, left and right (17.0→21.5,
eased), the camera slowly pulling back so the line runs off both edges. Captions: "lies on the border between two
countries", "the peak is technically split in half".

**21.60–22.80 Nepal.** The line turns white, Nepal fills blue from the bottom (a wipe that rises to the border, 0.2 s),
then the camera pulls back fast; a huge white tilted "Nepal" sweeps in from the bottom-left and shrinks into its label
spot (0.4→0.95 s).

**22.80–24.60 China.** China fills red (whole shape at once, slight scale-pop), "China" label in white on the red,
"Mt Everest" between them. The reference's China outline includes Aksai Chin and its India is the disputed outline: we
must use India's official map here.

**24.60–25.30 Map → globe.** Zoom-out with a radial blur (0.4–0.55 s), the flat map wraps onto a 3D globe in the
nebula; China (red) and Nepal (blue) stay painted on it.

**25.30–28.40 Globe.** Globe drifts and grows slightly; "Nepal" and "China" labels follow the shapes.
Captions: "closest to space are Nepal and China at the same time".

**28.40–30.20 "but that's wrong".** A small red circle with a white X pops on India (0.2 s), scales up to big
(0.27→0.4 s, overshoot), and the whole frame washes red (a red vignette/flash, 0.27→1.2 s), then the X shrinks back to
a dot and the red drains.

**30.20–32.40 "not actually round".** Globe spins (Pacific → Americas), shrinking and dimming.

**32.40–33.80 Flat-earth gag.** A flat-earth disc (ice wall rim, dome-ish map, brown underside) slides in from the
right and pushes the globe out (0.33→0.47 s). Holds on "it's flat".

**33.80–34.50 "Just kidding".** The disc slides out left, the globe comes back.

**35.20–36.90 "Sphere".** A white circle traces around the globe (ring draw-on, 0.5 s) and the word "Sphere" pops above
with a curved hand-drawn arrow pointing at the globe.

**36.60–37.40 Strike + retype.** A red strike line draws through "Sphere" (0.13 s), the letters scramble out and
"Geoid" types in letter by letter in a bouncy font (0.47→0.8 s), keeping the arrow.

**38.40–41.80 Rotation.** Globe spins faster on its axis ("because of its rotation on its axis").

**41.80–46.00 Bulge and squash.** Two white triangle arrows push outwards left and right at the equator (the globe
stretches wide, 42.0→43.5), then two arrows push down/up at the poles (squash, 44.0→45.5). The globe ends clearly
oblate (exaggerated about 1.3:1).

**46.20–50.60 Uneven mass.** A yellow glowing outline crawls round the globe's edge (46.5→48.0, starts top, runs
clockwise), wobbly/jagged like a potato silhouette; the globe beneath turns lumpy. "it isn't a perfect ellipsoid".

**50.60–59.20 The geoid model.** Hard cut to black: a lumpy, rainbow-coloured (blue low, red high) "potato" model of
the geoid, exaggerated, spinning and tumbling (two different renders, one flat 2D-ish disc with a colour bar at 54.5).
This is a third party's scientific visualisation in the reference: we need our own.

**59.20–64.40 Radius diagram.** The potato fades back to the real globe (0.3 s crossfade). A thin blue line rises from
the centre to the North Pole with "6,357 km" (blue pill label); a red line runs from the centre to the equator edge
with "6,378 km" (red pill). "+21 km" in glowing yellow pops above the red label at "21 kilometres".

**64.40–65.00 Equator dive.** The globe turns to Africa, a dashed white equator line appears, then a fast zoom with
radial blur onto the equator over East Africa.

**65.00–68.50 Hunt on the equator.** The magnifier comes back and slides west along the dashed equator, Africa →
Atlantic → South America; at "Mount Chimborazo" a red triangle and "Mt Chimborazo" label pop in the lens.

**68.50–72.50 Andes.** Lens drops away, camera zooms into the Andes (flat satellite with relief), label "Mt Chimborazo
6,263 m" (a second line pops at "over 6,000 meters").

**72.50–73.80 Ecuador.** Ecuador fills orange with a soft wipe (0.27→0.6 s); a big white tilted "Ecuador" sweeps in
and settles beside it (same move as "Nepal").

**73.80–75.00 Back to the globe.** Zoom-out with blur, the map wraps onto the globe again.

**75.00–81.20 Two distances.** From the globe's centre, a blue line grows to Everest's summit ("6,382 km") and a red
line to Chimborazo's ("6,384 km"); both summits are red triangles on the limb. "+2 km" in yellow glow pops above the red
label at "two kilometres farther".

**81.20–84.30 Answer card.** The globe turns to South America and grows; "Ecuador" types in letter by letter in
glowing gold (0.27→0.6 s), then "the closest to space" types under it, with a curved arrow to a glowing white flare on
Chimborazo. Hold to the end.

## Style rules to keep

- Captions: one short phrase at a time, white, small, centred low (~70% down), word-synced.
- Labels: rounded bold sans; numbers in pills (blue for polar, red for equatorial/answer); highlights glow (green =
  "seems right", red = wrong, yellow/gold = the real answer).
- Motion: everything eases with overshoot; reveals are draw-ons (lines, rings, strikes) and letter-by-letter types.
- Every scene change is a zoom with radial blur; the camera never stops drifting.
- Colour coding stays fixed: Nepal blue, China red, Ecuador orange/gold.

## Sources: what we use instead

- Flat map and Andes/Himalaya top-downs: EOX Sentinel-2 cloudless (CC BY 4.0, credit on screen).
- 3D Everest flyover: AWS Terrain Tiles (SRTM, open data) draped with EOX in our `Terrain3D` (as in Tibet). No Google
  Earth anywhere.
- Globe: our three.js globe (as in WW3/Bushby) with EOX texture.
- Geoid: our own exaggerated geoid from the EGM96 model (NGA, public domain), drawn as a displaced, colour-ramped
  sphere. Not the reference's model.
- Borders: Natural Earth India point-of-view edition. India in its official boundary whenever it is on screen,
  including the China fill (no Aksai Chin, no Arunachal in China).
- Flat-earth gag, magnifier, arrows, pills: drawn in code.

## Facts the reference states (to check in our script)

- Everest 8,848.86 m (2020 Nepal–China survey); the summit is on the Nepal–China (Tibet) border.
- Earth's equatorial radius 6,378.1 km, polar 6,356.8 km: about 21 km more at the equator.
- Chimborazo 6,263 m, Ecuador, about 1.5° south of the equator (near the equator, not on it).
- Distance from Earth's centre: Chimborazo summit about 6,384.4 km, Everest about 6,382.3 km: about 2 km farther.
- "Closest to space" in the reference means farthest from Earth's centre.
