# Reference analysis: "Why North and South Korea Are Still at War: Enemies After 70 Years" (88.1 s)

Measured from the file: 720×1280, 30 fps, 88.1 s, English VO (whisper medium, `reference_transcript.txt`). Scene
detection finds **no hard cuts**: the whole video is one satellite map of East Asia with the camera pushing, pulling
and panning. Pauses in the VO (silencedetect) at 3.0, 29.8, 42.1, 52.4, 58.1, 62.1 and 69.0 s mark the acts.

## The look

- One satellite base map (Sentinel-style, natural colour, relief), no grade change all video.
- Countries as flat fills with a thin white edge: North Korea red, South Korea blue, Japan's empire orange, the
  Korean Empire violet, USSR red, China dark red. Diagonal hatching on the big hero shapes at the start.
- **Flag badges** (round, white rim) sit on each side and move with the story: hammer-and-sickle on the North, US
  flag on the South, then DPRK/ROK flags, then the UN, US and Chinese badges stacking on the front line.
- **Glowing rings** (red, blue) pulse round the rival badges for "opposing ideologies".
- The **38th parallel** as a thin white line across the sea and a yellow segment across the peninsula, later a glowing
  red-orange line on the land border.
- Big labelled tags ("North Korea", "South Korea", "USSR", "Empire of Japan", "≈2M Chinese 'Volunteers'"); dates as
  yellow ticks on a timeline line ("1910 ——— 1945").
- Small props: a wall with barbed wire between the two flags, a white surrender flag, a helmet + AK icon.
- One-line captions, white, small, bottom third.
- **The front line is animated**: the blue fills almost all of Korea (Pusan), then red pushes back, then blue pushes
  up to the Chinese border, then the line settles back near the 38th parallel.

## Why it works

- **One map, no cuts.** The camera only moves, so 88 s feel like one continuous story.
- **Colour = side.** Every reader knows instantly who is who, even with the sound off.
- **The moving front line** (58–80 s) is the money shot: the war shown as a tide going down, up and back.
- Weak spots: a slow hook (a plain question, no image of the stakes until 3 s), 14 s of background before anything
  surprising, and a flat ending with no loop.

## Fact check of the reference

| Claim in the reference | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| No diplomatic relations | **True.** Both joined the UN in 1991, but they have no formal relations. |
| Divided by walls and barbed wire | **Mostly.** The DMZ (about 248 km long, about 4 km wide) is fences, barbed wire and minefields. There is no continuous wall; North Korea has added barriers and walls on its side since 2024. |
| Technically at war for decades | **True.** The 1953 armistice ended the fighting; no peace treaty has been signed. |
| Just 100 years ago one country, the Korean Empire | **Loose.** The Korean Empire lasted 1897–1910 (116 years ago). Korea stayed one territory, under Japan, until 1945 (81 years ago). |
| 1910, Japan annexed Korea | **True** (treaty of 22 August 1910, in force 29 August). |
| Under Japan until the end of WWII | **True** (to August/September 1945, 35 years). |
| Days before the surrender, Soviets entered from the north | **True.** The USSR declared war on 8 August 1945 and its troops entered northern Korea from about 12 August; Japan announced its surrender on 15 August. |
| …while the US advanced from the south | **Wrong.** No US troops were in Korea before the surrender. They landed at Incheon on 8 September 1945. |
| The US proposed dividing Korea at the 38th parallel | **True.** Two US Army colonels, Dean Rusk and Charles Bonesteel, picked the line on the night of 10–11 August 1945, working from a National Geographic map; Rusk later wrote that they had about 30 minutes. The USSR accepted. |
| A temporary solution for joint administration | **Roughly.** The line divided the zones for taking Japan's surrender. The plan (Moscow, December 1945) was a trusteeship of up to five years before independence. |
| Two countries, each claiming the whole peninsula | **True.** Republic of Korea, 15 August 1948; DPRK, 9 September 1948. Both claim all of Korea. |
| North Korea launched a war, nearly captured the whole South | **True.** The invasion began on 25 June 1950; by September the South held only the Pusan Perimeter. |
| US-led UN forces pushed back almost to the Chinese border | **True.** After the Incheon landing (15 September 1950), UN forces reached the Yalu in late October 1950. |
| China entered unofficially, about 2 million "volunteers" | **"Unofficially" true, the number is shaky.** China's troops fought as the "People's Volunteer Army" from October 1950. Figures differ: Chinese sources give about 2.9–3 million who served in total over the war, by rotation; peak strength at any one time was about 1.2–1.35 million. Our script says "लाखों" (hundreds of thousands to millions), which is safe. |
| The USSR provided military equipment and weapons | **True,** and Soviet pilots flew MiG-15s in secret. |
| The front moved back to near the 38th parallel; armistice; no peace treaty | **True.** Armistice of 27 July 1953, signed by the UN Command, North Korea and China. **South Korea did not sign it.** |
| "Enemies after 70 years" (title) | **73 years** in 2026 (1953 → 2026). |

Extra facts used in our draft:
- Deaths: estimates vary, but at least 2.5 million soldiers and civilians died (many sources give about 3 million).
  The script says "कम से कम 25 लाख".
- India: the 60th Parachute Field Ambulance (Lt Col A. G. Rangaraj) served with the UN in Korea from 1950 to 1954
  and treated many thousands of soldiers and civilians. After the armistice India chaired the Neutral Nations
  Repatriation Commission (Gen. K. S. Thimayya), with the Custodian Force India guarding the prisoners who refused to go
  home. (Optional beat; "हज़ारों" is the safe word.)

## What we can't take

The reference's map renders, flag-badge graphics, wall and helmet icons and captions. Our version uses the house
pipeline: EOX Sentinel-2 plate + AWS relief, Natural Earth borders (India POV edition, so India's official boundary
shows wherever India is in frame), flags drawn in code (national flags are public symbols; draw them, don't screenshot),
the 38th parallel and the front lines drawn from our own keyframes, the DMZ line from Natural Earth's border. Null in a
split screen in the middle only, 5–8 s.

## Our edge over the reference

1. A stronger hook: the claim first ("तुम्हें लगता है… खत्म हो गई? कभी नहीं हुई।"), with the DMZ on screen at frame 0.
2. The "two officers, about 30 minutes, one line" beat: the best detail in the story, which the reference skips.
3. The front line as a tide, as in the reference, but bigger and with the dates.
4. "South Korea didn't even sign the armistice": a true surprise near the end.
5. Optional India beat (60 Para Field Ambulance) for our audience.
6. A loop ending: "तो अब बताओ…" runs straight back into "तुम्हें लगता है…".
