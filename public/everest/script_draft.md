## Fact notes (checked 24 Nov 2026)

- **Everest height:** 8,848.86 m, the joint China–Nepal announcement of 8 Dec 2020 (Al Jazeera, NPR, China Daily). Said
  as 8,849.
- **Summit rock:** the Qomolangma Formation, marine limestone, Middle Ordovician (Darriwilian, about 467–458 million years),
  so "क़रीब 47 करोड़ साल". Deposited on the passive northern margin of India (NSF-funded study; IUGS Geoheritage).
- **Fossils:** brachiopods, crinoids, conodonts (IUGS Geoheritage). "Trilobites at the summit" was not found, so it's not
  used.
- **Collision:** India–Asia began about 50 million years ago, with a 40–60 Ma range across USGS, Eos and National
  Geographic, so "क़रीब पाँच करोड़". The Tethys closed between them (multiple sources).
- **Langza:** Spiti, Himachal, about 4,400 m (travel guides). Ammonites (Jurassic) from the Tethys (Outlook Traveller; a
  geologist's 2016 visit). Selling fossils is prohibited (reported), so nothing on screen shows collecting.
- **Still rising:** confirmed. Sources give rates from about 1 mm to over 1 cm a year, so no number is used.
- Every fossil and old-sea picture is an AI illustration or drawn in code and labelled. Terrain from AWS Terrain Tiles /
  SRTM (credited). India's official boundary on every map.
