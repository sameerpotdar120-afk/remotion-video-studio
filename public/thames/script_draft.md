## Fact notes (checked 28 Nov 2026)

- **1957, biologically dead:** surveys in 1957 found the tidal Thames could not sustain life; it was declared biologically
  dead (credited to the Natural History Museum). In the 1950s no fish were recorded along a 69 km stretch, Kew to
  Gravesend (Down To Earth / The Conversation, 2022; ScienceBlog).
- **Great Stink:** July–August 1858, a hot summer made the smell of the sewage-filled river unbearable; Parliament sits on
  the river and was pushed to act (Wikipedia; London Museum). The reference said "1958": wrong.
- **Bazalgette:** intercepting sewers along both banks carried sewage to outfalls east of the city; completed 1875. The
  1866 outbreak was London's last major cholera epidemic; his system is credited with ending them (Museum of London;
  Wikipedia). Four big epidemics: 1832, 1849, 1854, 1866.
- **Treatment and oxygen:** treatment upgraded at the big works; the Thames Bubbler (1988) and Thames Vitality (1999)
  can each add about 30 tonnes of oxygen a day after storm spills (Environment Agency blog; edie).
- **Super sewer:** Thames Tideway Tunnel, 25 km, fully operational Feb 2025; cost £4.6–5 billion (Thames Water; Wikipedia).
  At about ₹105–110 a pound that is roughly ₹48,000–55,000 crore, so "क़रीब पचास हज़ार करोड़".
- **Wildlife:** ZSL State of the Thames (2021): over 115 fish species in the tidal Thames, seahorses, grey and harbour
  seals, and sharks (tope, starry smooth-hound, spurdog). The same report flags sewage and climate risks, so we say
  "लौट आए हैं", not "perfectly clean".
- **Yamuna:** the 22 km Delhi stretch is about 2% of the river's length but carries about 80% of its pollution load
  (CSE; CEEW gives 79%); about 84% of Delhi's load comes from two drains, Najafgarh and Shahdara (CSE). Dissolved oxygen
  in midstream and downstream Delhi was nil in 2024 (CSE citing DPCC).
- **Left out on purpose:** "WW2 bombing broke the sewers" (from the reference): not confirmed by any source found, so the
  script says only that the city and its waste grew.
