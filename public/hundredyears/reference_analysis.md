# Reference: "France vs England: The Hundred Years' War / Joan of Arc"

The reference is 720×1280 at 30 fps and runs 97.6 s. The VO is English, a male narrator, about 2.7 words a second. Joan's and the English king's lines are voiced in character. The video closes on the war's end ("1337–1453") with no CTA.

**Method:**
- Analysed at 6 fps (585 frames) for the beats.
- Every key motion was then stepped through frame by frame at 30 fps, for the timing and the easing.
- Word timings come from faster-whisper medium. Its language detection broke at 28.7–46.9 s, so the on-screen captions fill that stretch.

## Look

### Two map bases

- **Satellite.** A flat top-down satellite map (green and brown land, deep teal sea, snowy Alps) for the hook, Joan's rise, and the close. It looks almost the same as our EOX grade.
- **Parchment.** A vintage relief map (tan land, slate-teal sea, coarse coastline, no labels) for the 1337 history section (13.2–22.2 s). The switch between the two is a hard cut.

### Territory fills

- **Colours:** England is a flat saturated red (#D0111B) and France a flat royal blue (#1F2BD0). There is no outline, no gradient and no sheen; they are plain flat fills.
- **Soft borders:** where the two meet near Joan, the colours melt through white (a red → white → blue gradient) under a radial white glow.
- **Territory changes:**
  - Red occupation flows across France with a soft, blobby leading edge (57.5–58.4 s).
  - Blue later spreads back.
  - At the end, banners pop all over as markers: St George's crosses for England, gold fleur-de-lis on blue for France.

### Characters

Chibi "plush toy" figures: big heads, stubby bodies, soft 3D shading. They are cut-outs pinned on the map, with an idle bob.

- **The two knights:**
  - An English great-helm knight with a Union Jack shield and a French bascinet knight with a tricolour shield. The flags are wrong for the era; see the fact flags.
  - They swing swords at each other in alternation at about 4 Hz.
- **The kings:** Edward III (crown, beard, ermine robe) and Philip VI.
- **Joan, in four looks:** as a peasant (brown dress, cap), in armour with a shield, mounted on a fleur-de-lis caparisoned horse with the white banner, and tied to the stake.
- **The others:** French knights on horses, English longbowmen, and a hooded judge with a scroll.
- **Stick limbs:** thin black lines are added on top of the plush bodies. The king's arms flail while he schemes (17.2 s); Joan's arms rise for "I was told from above".

### Text

- **Captions:** small white sans, one short phrase at a time, centred at about 71% height. Character lines are in italics.
- **Counter:** "13 → 116 years" rolls up in yellow digits plus white "years" (4.7–6.1 s). It then rushes at the camera, scaling up and off the top (6.8–7.4 s).
- **"1337":** big white heavy numerals slam in from the top-left. They are tilted, motion-blurred and oversized, settle in about 0.4 s (13.4–13.8), then punch again (14.8).
- **Map labels:** calligraphic serif small caps with a hand-drawn curved arrow that draws on: "Domrémy", "Siege of Orléans (1428–1429)", "Loire Campaign (1429)", "Reims (1429)", "Rouen 1431".
- **Character card:** a parchment name plate "Joan of Arc" with RPG stat icons beneath it: crown 0, swords 0, then a fist whose green bar fills ("just confidence").
- **Title card at the end:** "Hundred Years' War" in yellow over "1337–1453" in white.

### Camera

- **During holds:** never still. A constant slow push-in of about 3–5% a second, plus a slight drift.
- **Between beats:** fast snap zooms (about 0.2–0.4 s, ease-out with a small overshoot). Pull-outs reveal the bigger map; for example, 57.3–58.4 goes from a village close-up to all of France.

### Transitions

- **Iris:** a circle opens from Joan's feet on the map to reveal the village scene (45.8–46.1 s, 10 frames, ease-in).
- **Whip pan:** a blur from the knights' field to the archers (26.8–27.1).
- **Dissolve:** the village scene goes dark, then dissolves into a glowing satellite close-up (57.0–57.5).
- **Burn:** the stake scene burns away like paper. Orange glowing contour blotches eat the frame and reveal the map underneath, flags and all (88.9–90.8). This is the signature move.
- **Shockwave:** a white and cyan ring expands from Joan across France as the soldiers rally (58.5–60.4).

### Pacing

A new element or cut every 0.5–1.5 s, and nothing static for more than about 1 s.

## Fact flags (to check before our script)

- **England's extent:** the reference paints all of Great Britain and Ireland red. In 1337 the English crown held England, Wales and a lordship in Ireland; Scotland was a separate kingdom allied with France (the Auld Alliance). We show England and Wales, plus England's land in France: Gascony in 1337, and the north under the Treaty of Troyes in the 1420s.
- **Flags:** the Union Jack (1606/1801) and the tricolour (1790s) did not exist yet. Use St George's cross and the English royal arms (the leopards) for England, and gold fleur-de-lis on blue for France. The reference's own ending already does this.
- **"1337 … king of England … why not just become king of France too":** Edward III claimed the French throne through his mother Isabella after the last direct Capetian died in 1328. The war opened in 1337, when Philip VI confiscated Gascony. The joke is fine; the claim is real.
- **"For over 100 years", "116 years":** 1337–1453 is 116 years, of on-and-off war with long truces.
- **The longbow:** it was decisive at Crécy (1346) and Agincourt (1415).
- **"France falls into chaos, the king is weak":** Charles VI's madness and the Armagnac–Burgundian civil war. Burgundy sided with England, and the Treaty of Troyes (1420) made Henry V heir to France.
- **Joan:**
  - Born about 1412 at Domrémy, a peasant's daughter. She said saints' voices (Michael, Catherine, Margaret) told her to save France.
  - She reached the Dauphin at Chinon in 1429 and lifted the siege of Orléans in May 1429, after an arrow wound at Les Tourelles.
  - The Loire campaign followed in June 1429, including Patay, and Charles VII was crowned at Reims on 17 July 1429.
- **"The English kidnap her":** Burgundian forces captured her at Compiègne in May 1430 and sold her to the English. That needs a wording fix.
- **The trial:** a church trial at Rouen under Bishop Pierre Cauchon (January–May 1431), with charges that included heresy and wearing men's clothes. She was burned on 30 May 1431, aged about 19. The verdict was overturned in 1456 and she was canonised in 1920.
- **The end:** France won at Castillon in 1453. England kept only Calais, until 1558.
- **Sensitivity:** the burning stays cartoon and off-camera (the burn dissolve), as in the reference. Saints and religion stay respectful.
- **Game UI:** the 30.8–40.3 s section reuses a real strategy game's interface. We build our own UI in code and write our own chat lines.
