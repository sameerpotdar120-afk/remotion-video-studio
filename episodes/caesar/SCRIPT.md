# The Worst Hostage in History: script v1 (411 words, ~147 s at the creator's 1.10x pace)

Hook A (75 STRONG, The Superlative). Hero's name is held back until the final line.

You think you know the worst hostage in history, but in 75 BC these pirates almost lost their minds before he even left.
It starts near a tiny island in the Aegean Sea. A pirate fleet stops a ship and drags off one passenger. Twenty-five years old. Expensive clothes. Strangely calm.
The pirates name their price. Twenty talents of silver. A fortune. They expect him to beg.
Instead, he laughs in their faces. Twenty? You clearly have no idea who you've caught. Ask for fifty.
Fifty talents. That's over a tonne of silver.
So he sends his people off to the nearby cities to collect it, and stays behind with just one friend and two servants. Alone, with some of the most dangerous men at sea.
And this is where it gets weird. He doesn't act like a prisoner. He acts like their boss.
When he wants to sleep, he sends a servant to tell the pirates to keep it down.
He writes poems and speeches, then makes the pirates sit and listen. And if they don't clap, he calls them illiterate barbarians, right to their faces.
And again and again, with a smile, he promises them: when I get out of here, I'm going to crucify every single one of you.
The pirates think it's hilarious. They love this kid. They think he's joking.
Thirty-eight days later, the ransom arrives. He's free.
Any normal person would run home and never look at the sea again. Not him.
He goes straight to the port of Miletus, raises ships and men, and sails right back to that island.
The pirates are still there. Still anchored. Probably still counting his silver.
He captures most of them, takes the money back, and locks them up in the prison at Pergamon.
But there's a problem. Punishing them is the governor's job, and the governor has his eye on all that silver. He says he'll think about it.
So our hostage doesn't wait. He races back to the prison before the governor can decide.
He drags the pirates out and does exactly what he promised on that island. Every one of them goes on a cross.
But because they'd treated him well, he had their throats cut first, so they wouldn't suffer up there.
The pirates thought they'd kidnapped a rich kid. They'd actually kidnapped the man who would one day rule Rome.
That hostage was a twenty-five-year-old Julius Caesar. And he always kept his promises.
