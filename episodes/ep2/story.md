## Voice: paste everything between the lines
---
If you put the only tree for 20 miles beside a dead-straight road, you'll never stop people wrecking their cars on it. Here's why.
You see, the road is flat, empty, and straight as a ruler. And right at the edge stands one tree.
Yet every week, somebody plows into it.
So the town bolts a giant glowing arrow to the trunk, so nobody can miss it.
The crashes double.
Our guy, the local tow truck driver, sets up a lawn chair beside it. Business has never been better.
Finally, the sheriff drives out to see it. He creeps along at 15 miles an hour, both hands glued to the wheel, and smacks straight into it.
He storms out, grabs a chainsaw off the tow truck, and cuts the tree down, while our guy watches his paycheck hit the dirt.
The day his cruiser is fixed, the sheriff heads back out to enjoy his empty road, and slams right into the stump.
So how does this keep happening?
Well, it's called target fixation.
After miles of nothing, that tree stands out like a lighthouse. Add a glowing arrow, and it's impossible to ignore.
Every driver's eyes lock onto it and won't let go. And while they stare, their hands quietly steer the car toward it.
Even when it's just a stump.
---

## Facts
- **Target fixation:** real and well documented. People steer toward the thing they stare at; pilots, motorcyclists and drivers are trained against it ("look where you want to go").
- **The lone tree story:** a fable told as an example. The town, the sheriff and our guy are illustrative characters, not a real event.
