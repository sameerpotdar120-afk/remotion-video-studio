# 04 · Case study: the long-form story reference ("rails need gaps")

The creator's reference for video #4: 65.2 s, 1080x1920, 30 fps. Story: a young engineer leaves gaps between rails and is
mocked; a rival lays a perfect gapless line and wins at first; summer heat closes his gaps and buckles the rival's line under a
train. Private study only; the lines are not reproduced here.

## 1. Script structure (word-timed transcript)
| segment | time | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| hook | 0-3.7 s | cold open on the strange act + an exact number (a gap every 39 ft). No title, no intro |
| setup | 3.7-11.5 s | stakes: a race, winner takes everything |
| conflict | 11.5-21.6 s | the boss explodes: his face literally fills with colour and steams |
| rival wins | 21.6-36 s | the perfect line is smooth and silent, ours is noisy. Public mockery (a newspaper), everyone switches |
| turn | ~36 s | "then summer comes": the gaps shrink, measured with everyday props (coin → sheet of paper) and a calendar |
| false victory | 44-50 s | the boss gloats. Dramatic irony: the viewer already suspects |
| payoff | 50-65 s | the perfect rails strain in the heat and kick sideways as a train arrives. Disaster. Hard end mid-sentence (loop) |

- **Pace and length:** 218 words in 64.9 s = **3.36 w/s**.
- **The science is never named or lectured.** It is shown through the gap sizes, a colour heat map on the rails, and the buckle.
- Characters carry it: hero (flat cap, vest), boss (bowler, suit), rival (top hat, luxury carriage), a newsboy.

## 2. Picture (measured)
- **Cuts:**
  - only **14 hard cuts** in 65 s (15 shots, median 1.2 s between them);
  - transitions are mostly camera-move dissolves (295 frames in soft transitions);
  - cuts sit **−5..+5 f from a word onset**, usually 1-4 f *before* the word.
- **Camera:**
  - still on only **4 %** of frames;
  - median motion 3.3 px/f horizontal, 3.1 px/f vertical (at 1080);
  - zoom 0.24 %/f, roll 0.19°/f: lively, with fast push-ins and whip pans.
- **Look:**
  - luma mean 0.45, saturation 0.49 (very saturated);
  - red/orange canyon walls against a strong blue sky, then sunset purple/orange for the finale.
- **Sets:** about 6 in 65 s (canyon trackside, trestle bridge, grand station, carriage interior, carriage exterior, sunset bridge).
- **Action timing:**
  - colour-fill face: starts on the noun, completes ~5 f early; steam lands on the colour word;
  - rail buckle: +4 f after its verb, sparks on the next word;
  - explosion lands on the verb of the train arriving.
  - Rule: **actions on or slightly before the word, never late.**
- **Framing:** big face close-ups (face ~40 % of frame) for emotion beats; wides for the world.

## 3. Mechanism shown as visuals (the part to copy)
1. **Heat as colour:** the rails shade from blue/magenta (cool) through pink and orange to yellow and white-hot as the season advances.
2. **Gap gauges:** everyday props in the gap (a coin, then paper that barely fits) with the month visible in frame.
3. **Strain before failure:** a glow ripples along the perfect rails, with groaning sound.
4. **The failure:** an S-curve in ~3-5 frames with sparks, then the train tips sideways.
5. **Consequence:**
   1. a wide shot of the train leaving the bridge;
   2. a huge fireball for ~1 s (white core → orange → brown smoke);
   3. a cut to characters with dropped jaws and a hat flying.

## 4. Sound
- -14.1 LUFS integrated, LRA 3.2 LU.
- Continuous tonal music bed ~9 dB under the voice.
- Sparse SFX: saw strokes at the open, a whoosh on the first cut, a train, then the climax cluster (buckle, crash,
  explosion) in the last 7 s.

## 5. What we took (rules for long-form story shorts)
1. Tell a **story with characters and stakes**; the science is the twist, shown not lectured. One short "because" line at
   the end is allowed for clarity.
2. **Make the invisible visible:** colour-code the hidden quantity, measure it with everyday props, show the time passing.
3. **Plant → mock → pay off:** the hero is doubted, the rival wins first, nature settles it.
4. 60-90 s is fine for this format. The VO can be faster than our own voice (ElevenLabs ~3.2-3.4 w/s, or speed it up, see `05`).
5. Actions on or up to 5 f before the word.
6. One hero disaster at the end, then a reaction shot, then a hard cut that loops.

## 6. Our original version (video #4)
- **Story:** a desert town gets its first train. Our mascot leaves a gap every 39 ft and is fired by the mayor. The new crew
  lays the rails tight; gloating follows. On the Fourth of July the gapless line kinks, and our guy flags the train down.
- **Props:** a pencil as the gap gauge, plus a thermometer.
- **Ending:** a rescue, not a crash; the last line loops into the hook. All lines and characters are original.
- **Facts used:**
  - 39 ft was the US standard length for jointed rail;
  - steel grows ~11.7 µm per m per °C, so a 39 ft (11.9 m) rail grows ~7.6 mm over a 55 °C swing, about a pencil's width;
  - "sun kink" is the real term; modern welded rail is laid pre-stressed at a neutral temperature.
