# Hook playbook (narration + visual), from Nov 17

The creator: "we are doing good but we are still messed up in the visual and narration hook." This is the audit of the
last five hooks and the rules every NullDynasty script and edit follows from now on. A Short is kept or swiped in the
first 1–2 seconds, so everything here is about what is heard and seen before 0:03.

The same five mistakes, in every one except Ram Setu:

1. **Setup before the twist.** We spend 2.5–5 s on a date, a history line or "you never notice" before the surprising
   part. Ram Setu, the only one that puts the twist in the first 3 s, is the one to copy.
2. **Three claims, not one.** Every hook stacks a second and third fact ("…और 1801 में bribe…", "…और 1964 में
   समुंदर…", "…और 25 करोड़ साल बाद…"), so the opening runs 10 s and the best fact gets diluted.
3. **The same ending every time.** All five end on "कैसे?/क्यों?/और क्या होता है?" at about 10 s. Regular viewers learn
   the template; a generic question is not an open loop.
4. **Frame 0 is a word, not a picture.** "1947", "अंग्रेज़", a stopwatch. The thing people care about (Somnath, the
   four countries, the Setu) shows up seconds later. With the sound off, frame 0 says nothing.
5. **Voice and picture are not on the same thing.** In Junagadh the voice says "Gujarat" while the screen shows "1947";
   Somnath is heard at 5.4 s. The eye and the ear should land on the same noun at the same moment.

The Mumbai Short (about 3M views) opened with the place everyone knows, turned upside down, in one line under 6 s, on
a frame 0 that every Indian recognises.

## Narration rules

1. **The known thing first.** The first two words are the place, temple, person or object people already know (सोमनाथ,
   मुंबई, राम सेतु, ताज महल). No dates, no "Kya aap jaante hain", no "1947 में" as the first word. Dates go in sentence two.
2. **The twist inside 2.5 seconds** (about 8 words at our pace of 2.5–3 words a second). Write the first sentence so
   the surprise is in it, not after it.
3. **One claim.** The hook is one sentence plus at most one short sentence. The other facts move to 0:08 and later,
   where they become the "and it gets worse" beats that hold the middle.
4. **No fixed question tag.** Don't end the hook with "कैसे?" by habit. Either open a specific loop that only the
   ending closes ("…और जिसने इसे वापस लिया, उसका बेटा बाद में Pakistan का Prime Minister बना।"), or go straight into
   the story with no question at all.
5. **Make it about "तुम" or "हम".** Ram Setu ("तुम… जा सकते थे") and the Somnath ending ("जिस सोमनाथ के दर्शन तुम…")
   are our strongest lines. Use that shape in the opening, not only at the end.
6. **Something felt, not only a fact.** Pride, loss, injustice or disbelief, and for Hindu-audience topics, aastha.
7. **Loop-ready.** The last line runs into the first, so the first line has to make sense straight after the last one.

## Visual rules (the first 3 seconds)

1. **Frame 0 is the payoff picture, not a title.** It shows the known thing *and* the contradiction, readable with the
   sound off: Somnath sitting inside a Pakistan-green Junagadh; the UK splitting into four colours; people walking on
   the Setu. Overlay text at most 3 words, never a big title covering the picture.
2. **Already moving on frame 0.** Start mid-push, mid-zoom or mid-wipe. No fade-in, no settling first second.
3. **A change every 0.5–0.8 s until 0:03**, each on a stressed word: a cut, a punch-in, a highlight wiping in, a
   person rising, a number flipping. About 4–5 changes before 0:03.
4. **Say-show sync.** Every noun in the hook appears on screen on its own word (the temple on "सोमनाथ", Pakistan's
   green on "Pakistan"). Check against the word timings, not by eye.
5. **Captions from frame 0**, big and short (3 words), never covering the subject.
6. **The person, early.** If the story has a famous person (Patel, Nehru, the Nawab), let them rise out of the map
   in the first 3 s, not at 0:40.
7. **Sound from frame 0.** A hit or riser on frame 0 and on the twist word; the music doesn't fade in.

## How we work from now on

1. **Before the VO is recorded,** with every script: 5 hook lines written to the narration rules, and for the best 3,
   a *frame-0 mock* (a real still rendered from the edit) and a 3-second storyboard (what changes on which word). The
   creator picks one; then the VO is recorded.
2. **The edit is built from frame 0 outward:** the first 3 s are built and checked (mute test, say-show check against
   the word timings) before the rest of the video.
3. **Measure, don't guess.** After each upload the creator shares YouTube Studio's *"Viewed vs. swiped away"* for the
   Short (and the retention graph's first 5 s). They go in the ledger below, so we learn which hook shapes work for
   this channel. hookscore.py (now Hinglish-aware) is only a rough check: by its own notes it barely separates a
   creator's hits from their misses.

## Example: the Junagadh hook, rewritten by these rules

- **Line:** "सोमनाथ मंदिर, कुछ हफ़्तों के लिए Pakistan का था। और Pakistan का नक्शा आज भी यही कहता है।"
  (the known thing first, the twist by 2 s, one claim plus one escalation, no "कैसे?", and the last line
  "…Pakistan का Map आज भी कहता है…" runs straight into it).
- **Frame 0:** the Somnath temple on the black-and-white coast, mid push-in, Junagadh already Pakistan-green around
  it, with the white outline wiping in.
- **0.0–3.0 s:** "सोमनाथ" (temple, hit) → "Pakistan" (green floods Junagadh, the crescent appears) → "कुछ हफ़्तों"
  (a "15.9 → 9.11.1947" counter flips) → "नक्शा आज भी" (pull out to Pakistan's 2020 map hatch).
