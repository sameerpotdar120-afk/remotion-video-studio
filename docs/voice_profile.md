# NullDynasty: voice profile

Inferred from the creator's own writing and recordings: the Super El Niño script they wrote themselves (Oct 2026), plus their recorded voiceovers for Darién, Diomede v2, North Sentinel and Super El Niño. Update this file if the creator corrects anything.

- **Language:** spoken Hindi with natural Hinglish. English nouns stay English but are written in Devanagari when spoken that way (पैसिफिक ओशन, ट्रेड विंड्स, AC). Numbers as digits (13 प्रतिशत, 1876, 2027).
- **Opening:** straight into the image or the claim, often with a command to imagine: "सोचो, …". No "दोस्तों", no channel intro, no "आज हम बात करेंगे".
- **Devices:** one everyday metaphor carried through the video (Pacific's "AC"); short punchy clauses; "बस …" to land a consequence; "और अब …" to bring it to today.
- **Certainty:** dramatic but hedged where the evidence is a forecast: "बन सकता है", "हो सकता है". Never claims a forecast as fact.
- **Audience:** Indian viewers; an India link is welcome when it is real (monsoon, famine numbers).
- **Close:** a direct question to the viewer, not a subscribe pitch ("क्या हम रेडी हैं?").
- **Length:** 70–100 s, about 2.6 Hindi words per second at their recording pace.
- **The creator rewrites scripts they don't like.** Hand over drafts as proposals; their final wording wins.
- **Respect for communities:** when a community is named, speak about them with warmth and respect, as "हमारे … भाई" (e.g. "हमारे कोली भाई"). Never make a people sound passive, primitive or like a footnote; show what was done to them, not what they "became".
- **Local facts the creator knows first-hand are stated plainly** (e.g. Mumbai's name comes from Mumba Devi, the Koli community's goddess). Don't hedge them with "माना जाता है"; hedge only real forecasts or genuinely disputed numbers.

## What the creator's Instagram data says (13 Oct 2026, 8 reels; see exports/instagram_analysis_oct13.md)

- **Shares decide reach.** Both reels past 200K views (Mumbai, El Niño) were shared by 1.4–4.9% of viewers; every reel under 130K by 0.6% or less. Pick topics a viewer would send to someone: about India, the viewer's own city, language or daily life, or a danger to them. Far-away "cool facts" get likes, not sends, and few follows (North Sentinel: 0.14 follows per 1,000 viewers).
- **Frame 0 = one clear picture + at most 3–4 big words.** Reels that opened on a sentence, wordplay or jargon skipped 26–32%; picture-first openings skipped 17–20%. The voice asks the full question; on-screen text builds after the first second.
- **No dates or background before the first payoff.** Pay part of the hook off by 8–10 s. Never two dates in back-to-back lines (Märket's cliff at "फिर 1885 में"; Sugar's drop at "करीब 1500 साल पहले").
- **Comments are near zero.** Alternate the close between a one-word-answer question and a send prompt tied to identity, and compare.
- **Length 60–90 s is fine** (Louisiana at 112 s had the lowest skip rate).

## Weekly mix: REACH and CONNECT (creator's rule, 13 Oct 2026)

14 videos a week. Every topic or reference the creator sends gets two scores out of 10, and a tag:
- **Reach:** hook strength, picture-first frame 0, a shock that works with zero context, any topic (Indian or not). Goal: virality and new viewers.
- **Connect:** would someone send it to friends or family, does it touch the viewer's identity or daily life, would they save it. Goal: shares, saves, comments, loyal followers.
- Tag REACH, CONNECT or BOTH; keep a running weekly count, about 7 and 7, with as many BOTH as possible.
- Even a REACH video gets a small hook to the viewer (an Indian angle, a "हमारे" moment, a comparison to India), because pure-shock reach brings few follows (North Sentinel: 92K viewers, 13 follows) while Mumbai went viral and connected (684 follows).
- **Don't force India in.** A pure journey or world story (REACH) is told on its own terms, following the reference's flow. Add an India angle only where it is real and asked for; no filler connections (Google's age, "Kashmir to Kanyakumari" comparisons, family lines the reference doesn't have).

## Null, the masked host (from Oct 13)

The channel has a recurring host character: "Null", a masked man (matte black full-face mask, upward almond eyes glowing amber, one thin gold line from the left temple curving below the eyes to the right jaw, black hoodie with the hood always up, black gloves, joggers, lace-up boots). The creator made him in Gemini; reference images are the creator's own AI art.
- For every video, write Google Omni prompts for 10-second clips (Omni's max) of Null sitting in a chair in the same fixed studio, doing hand gestures only (the mask has no expressions). I cut the clips into the main video wherever they fit the VO.
- Keep the studio, chair, lighting and camera identical in every clip. Start each clip from the same seated studio master image.
- He never speaks on camera (no lip-sync needed); the VO plays over him. Disclose as AI.
- Omni prompt rules (Gemini Omni Flash is under Google's Generative AI Prohibited Use Policy; its filters over-block and Google doesn't say which word tripped them): describe Null as a stylised 3D animated podcast host/character in a costume mask. Never use "masked man", "mysterious", "hidden", "hood up", "gloves", "glowing eyes" or other words that read as a criminal or threat. Let the attached master image carry the look. Keep prompts short and positive (few "never/no" lines), gestures friendly and explanatory, and say "animated character" and "podcast studio".
- Workflow with Null (from Oct 13): reference → analysis → script confirmed → TWO separate md files: (1) asset prompts, (2) Omni clip prompts for Null, each clip tied to the script lines where he appears, starting from public/null/studio_master_9x16.jpg. The creator then sends assets, Omni clips and VO together. Mute Omni audio. Crop each clip ~15% tighter from the top centre to drop the Gemini sparkle (bottom right, x 864–935, y 1704–1775 at 1080x1920); measure it on every new clip. Keep the AI labels on.
- Null on screen (creator's rule): about 5–8 s per Short, never more than 10 s in total. Only in the middle or at the end with the CTA, NEVER at the start (the hook is visuals only). Layout when he appears: split screen, main video in the top half, Null in the bottom half (crop the clip to a 1080x960 band around the mask and hands, which also drops the watermark).
- 4K from Oct 14: every video renders 4K 60 fps (Remotion composition at 60 fps, --scale=2, map canvas res 2, satellite plates one zoom level finer). Asset prompts ask for 4K output: Gemini Nano Banana Pro at 4K on a plain white background (I cut it out), or ChatGPT at its largest size (I upscale). The 4K master goes out through Git LFS (too big for a normal GitHub file or a direct send).
- Null screen time scales with length (creator, Oct 14): Shorts under ~70 s get 5–8 s; a ~2-minute video gets 10–15 s total, split over 2–3 moments in the middle and at the end (never the start).

## Asset look from Oct 14 (creator's rule): Unreal Engine 5, built in parts

- The GPT/Gemini props looked like cheap AI props. From now on every asset prompt asks for an **Unreal Engine 5 cinematic render**: Lumen-style global illumination, physically based materials, real surface detail (wood grain, rust, cloth weave, wet stone), ray-traced soft shadows, photoreal with a light stylised touch. Not "glossy miniature", not clay, not cartoon.
- **Every prop that moves is made in parts** so Remotion can rig and animate it (example: Kalpasar's dam gates should have actually swung shut and open, like the reference). Method: generate the HERO (assembled) first, then in the same chat ask for each moving part alone, same camera, angle, scale and lighting, plus a BASE with that part removed and the hole filled. I cut, align and pivot them.
- Fixed camera for a set: three-quarter view from about 30 degrees above, every part fully in frame, plain white background, no floor shadow (I add shadows in code).
- Editing to match: parts move on hinges and pivots (doors, gates, wheels, lids, scrolls unrolling, cannons recoiling), with 2.5D parallax, motion blur, light sweeps, dust/smoke/water particles and contact shadows drawn in code.
- **Asset prompt file format (creator, Oct 15):** only the image prompts and the style, nothing else. No beat maps, rules, checklists or explanations. One line of style + size at the top, then for each image its file name and one self-contained, paste-ready prompt (style written into every prompt). Each part is its own standalone prompt (not "same image, show only X"). Keep the count low: reuse one part several times (one hut, one bale, one door mirrored) instead of asking for near-duplicates. Don't ask again for images already received.
- **Editing style follows the reference exactly (creator, Oct 15, Tibet):** same scene order, same kind of moves and transitions as the reference, but every prop is our UE5 asset and every copyrighted source (Google Earth, Flightradar24) is rebuilt from free data and code.

## Render spec from Oct 15 (creator, overrides the 4K rule above)

- No more 4K. Every video renders **1080×1920 at 60 fps** (Remotion composition at 60 fps, scale 1). 4K took too long.
- Edit like a Premiere Pro editor, not an AI slideshow. UE5 image overlays and Veo video clips the creator generates are added on top of the GPT props (not instead of them): sometimes as overlays, sometimes full screen. The creator first marks where each goes on the timed transcript.

## Veo and prompt rules (creator, Oct 15)

- **Prompts are detailed and JSON-style** (shot, subject, action, camera, lighting, style, background, framing, aspect ratio, duration, avoid). More detail gives more accurate results. Applies to image and Veo prompts.
- **Veo props and effects: 16:9** so nothing gets cropped (the first ship test cut off the bow and stern). Full-screen Veo shots: 9:16. The whole prop in frame with empty space around it.
- Veo has no real transparency (it paints a fake checkerboard). I choose the background: chroma green #00FF00 for solid props, pure black for effects (smoke, mist, dust, fire, light). Veo props animate in place; I move them across the map.
- **Maps get 3D cut-out highlights:** regions extruded out of the satellite map with side walls, bevel, drop shadow and a camera tilt, like After Effects or Premiere, not flat glows.
- Received for Kolkata: veo/ship (17th-century ship on a painted checkerboard, 4K 24 fps, 4 s). Don't ask for it again.
