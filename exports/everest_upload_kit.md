## Fact notes (checked 22–24 Nov 2026)

- Everest 8,848.86 m: China–Nepal joint announcement, 8 Dec 2020. Said and shown as 8,849.
- The summit rock is the Qomolangma Formation: marine limestone, Middle Ordovician (about 467–458 million years), so
  "क़रीब 47 करोड़ साल". Laid down on India's passive northern margin (NSF-funded study; IUGS Geoheritage).
- Fossils in it: brachiopods, crinoids, conodonts (IUGS). Trilobites are not claimed.
- India–Asia collision began about 50 million years ago (40–60 Ma across USGS, Eos, National Geographic), so "क़रीब 5 करोड़".
- Langza, Spiti: about 4,400 m, Jurassic ammonites from the Tethys (Outlook Traveller). Selling them is reported as banned;
  the video says so and shows no collecting.
- Still rising: confirmed; rates vary by source, so the video gives no number.
- On screen: the old-sea map is labelled "चित्रण · क़रीब 47 करोड़ साल पहले" and the drift "सरल चित्रण"; the 470-million-year
  sea is not called Tethys (only the closing sea is). Everest's terrain is real (SRTM), slightly smoothed at 30 m.
