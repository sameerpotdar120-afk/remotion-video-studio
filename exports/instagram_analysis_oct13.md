# Instagram data: where we're losing, and what to change

Data: the creator's Reel Insights screenshots (13 Oct 2026), 8 reels. Per-viewer rates are worked out from the counts.
Instagram's own panel lists the rates "in order of importance to reach": skip rate first, then share rate, then likes, saves, reposts, comments.

Eight reels is a small sample and topics differ, so these are strong patterns, not proof.

## 1. Shares decide how far a reel goes, not watch time

- Both reels past 200K views were shared by more than 1 in 100 viewers (Mumbai 1.4%, El Niño 4.9%).
- Every reel under 130K was shared by fewer than 1 in 160 (0.6% or less).
- Diomede (60%) and Darién (57%) held viewers best and still stayed small, because almost nobody sent them.
- El Niño's views grew slowly for two days, then shot up on day three. That's what a reel looks like when it travels through DMs.
- Sugar has the second-best share rate (2.1%) and is only a day or two old. Don't judge it yet.

## 2. What people send vs what they only like

**Sent:** El Niño (a danger to India's monsoon: "send this to family"), Mumbai (your own city: "tag your Mumbai friend"), Sugar (words from every Indian kitchen: "send it to the friend who says चीनी"). All three are about the viewer's own life.

**Liked, not sent:** North Sentinel, Diomede, Darién, Märket. These are amazing facts about places the viewer has no tie to. North Sentinel reached 92K people and won only 13 follows (0.14 per 1,000). Shock content brings viewers who never come back.

**Who follows:** Sugar (2.3 per 1,000), Louisiana (1.8) and Mumbai (1.3), the language, India and history-of-us stories. That's the channel's real audience.

## 3. The hook: our share-worthy reels have our worst skip rates

- **Low skip (17–20%):** Louisiana, Diomede, Darién, North Sentinel. Each opens on a picture you understand in a split second, plus one or two words: three cartoon leaders at a map, "4 किमी" between two glowing coasts, "सड़क यहीं ख़त्म" with a skull, an archer's silhouette.
- **High skip (26–32%):** Mumbai, El Niño, Sugar, Märket. Each opens on a sentence you have to read or decode: "मुंबई कभी एक शहर था ही नहीं!", "Super El Niño" (jargon), "चीनी = चीन? मिश्री = मिस्र?" (wordplay), "ये बॉर्डर ज़िग-ज़ैग क्यों है?" (a place nobody knows).

**The fix:** keep the share-worthy Indian topic, and open the way the low-skip reels do. Frame 0 is one clear picture and at most 3–4 big words. The full question comes from the voice, and the text builds after the first second.

## 4. The first 15 seconds: we explain too early

- Every curve loses 25–55% of viewers before 0:15.
- **Sugar** drops to about 45% by 0:15, right as "करीब 1500 साल पहले, भारत ने गन्ने के रस को…" starts. History arrives before any payoff.
- **Märket** has a visible cliff at about 0:30, on "फिर 1885 में", its second date in a row ("कहानी 1809 की है… फिर 1885 में").
- **Mumbai** paid off its hook by 0:08 ("ये सात द्वीप थे"), then named places viewers know.

**Rule:** no dates or background before the first payoff. Deliver part of the answer by 8–10 s, and never put two dates in back-to-back lines.

## 5. Nobody comments

- Comments are about 0.0% on every reel. Mumbai had 56 comments from 529K viewers.
- The closing question isn't working as a comment driver. Shares count more for reach anyway.
- **Test:** alternate two endings and compare.
  - A one-word answer question: "अपने शहर का नाम लिखो".
  - A send prompt tied to identity: "उस दोस्त को भेजो जो…".

## 6. Length is not the problem

Louisiana runs 112 s and had the lowest skip rate and the longest average watch (61 s). Its reach was small because of its topic and low shares, not its length. The 60–90 s range is fine.

## What this means for Cooch Behar (not uploaded yet)

- **Topic:** strong on the share test. It's India, real people, and a choice every viewer will argue with, and Bengal viewers have a personal tie to it.
- **Risk is frame 0:** it opens on three lines of text ("क्या आप जानते हैं? / भारत के 111 गाँव / BANGLADESH के अंदर?"), which is the high-skip pattern. A cheap fix: frame 0 shows the map with the 111 glowing dots and only "111 गाँव" big. The rest builds on the voice from 0:01.
- **First payoff:** "सोए एक देश में, जागे दूसरे में" lands by 0:10, which is good.
