# Why the Mumbai reel went viral, and what to make next

Mumbai seven islands Short: about 700k views and 500+ followers on Instagram, our best by far.
Data used: our own scripts, hook scores (`hookscore.py`), VO lengths, and public view counts of competitor Shorts (yt-dlp, read-only, 12 Oct 2026).
Instagram Insights (shares, saves, reach from non-followers) were not available, so the "why" below is a reasoned read. Insights would confirm it.

## 2. What Mumbai had that the others didn't

1. **It's about the viewer's own country.** It's about the city of 2 crore people that every Indian knows. Every other video was about a foreign place. Our audience is Indian and Hindi-speaking.
2. **A shock about something familiar.** "मुंबई कभी एक शहर था ही नहीं" upends what people think they already know. That's stronger than an amazing fact about somewhere they've never been.
3. **Names people recognise.** Colaba, Worli, Mahim, Parel, Bandra-Worli: viewers find their own area in the story. People comment their neighbourhood and tag friends.
4. **People, pride and emotion.** "हमारे कोली भाई", Mumba Devi, and the ending, "सबसे ज़्यादा किसने खोया?", give it pride, respect and a sense of injustice. That's what drives shares and comments, not only views.
5. **Short punchy lines.** It has 28 very short lines at about 2.5 words a second, which made it easy to follow without sound.

In one line: **an Indian place + "you didn't know this about it" + a real story with real people + an emotional question at the end.**
