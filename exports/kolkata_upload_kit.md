## Fact notes

- **Three villages:** Kolkata grew from Sutanuti, Gobindapur and Kalikata on the east bank of the Hooghly. Sutanuti is usually placed around today's Bagbazar, Shobhabazar and Chitpur; Kalikata around BBD Bag and Burrabazar (central Kolkata); Gobindapur on the site of the Maidan and Fort William. The areas on screen are approximate.
- **Sutanuti's name:** the usual reading is a market for cotton thread bales (सूत + नुटि). Taught as the standard derivation, not a disputed claim.
- **Kalikata's name:** "कहा जाता है": the link to Kali (and Kalighat) is the most common explanation, but there are others, so the video hedges it.
- **1698 and ₹1,300:** in 1698 the East India Company acquired the zamindari rights to the three villages from the Sabarna Roy Choudhury family. ₹1,300 is the figure commonly cited for the deal. The video follows the creator's wording ("1,300 रुपये सालाना किराए पर"), and some accounts describe the payment differently, so the description doesn't repeat "yearly".
- **Gobindapur cleared for Fort William:** after the Battle of Plassey (1757), the Company built the new Fort William on Gobindapur's land and the residents were moved out.
- **Capital of British India:** Calcutta was the capital of British India until 1911, when the capital moved to Delhi.
- **Job Charnock and the High Court:** in 2003 the Calcutta High Court accepted an expert committee's finding that Job Charnock should not be called Kolkata's founder, since people lived there long before he arrived in 1690.
- **India:** shown with its official boundary (Natural Earth, India point-of-view edition).
