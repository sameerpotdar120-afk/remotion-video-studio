## Fact notes (checked 22 October 2026)

- **"सुबह बारिश, दोपहर कोहरा, शाम को धूप, 10 मिनट बाद ओले":** an illustration of how fast British weather can change, not a record of one real day.
- **"Scotland के कुछ हिस्सों में साल के 250 से ज़्यादा दिन बारिश":** commonly given for much of the Highlands as days with measurable rain (0.2 mm or more), falling to about 175 days on the Angus, Fife and East Lothian coasts. The figure appears in VisitScotland and other secondary sources that cite Met Office averages; the Met Office's station averages are the primary source.
- **"70 से ज़्यादा देशों में Britain से ज़्यादा बारिश":** in the World Bank's AG.LND.PRCP.MM table, 73 countries have a higher long-term average than the UK (about 1,220 mm a year), which ranks 74th. The tags in the video are those 73 countries.
- **"पहले नंबर पर Colombia, 3,240 मिलीमीटर":** Colombia tops the same table at 3,240 mm a year. The comparison bars use the same two numbers.
- **"वजह है गर्म मौसम, बाजू में Pacific Ocean, और ऊँचे पहाड़":** tropical heat, moist Pacific air pushed onto the land, and the three ranges of the Andes forcing it to rise and rain. The cordillera ribbons are drawn approximately.
- **"पश्चिम से … Gulf Stream":** strictly, the Gulf Stream (and its extension, the North Atlantic Drift) is a warm ocean current; it warms and moistens the westerly winds that bring Britain most of its rain (tropical and polar maritime air). The video uses the current as shorthand for that wet westerly air.
- **"उत्तर से Arctic की ठंडी हवा, दक्षिण से गर्म हवा, पूरब से Europe की सूखी हवा":** these match the Met Office's air masses for the British Isles: Arctic maritime from the north, tropical continental from the south (North Africa, mostly in summer), and polar continental from the east (central Europe, Scandinavia or Russia; cold and dry, mostly in winter). Polar maritime, from the north-west, is the most common.
- **"तो उस दिन जो हवा जीतती है, वही Britain का मौसम":** a simplification. Which air mass sits over Britain, and the fronts between them, largely sets the day's weather.
